Overview

A Robinson Crusoe
Chimp Encounter

Founded in the 1990s by safari legend Zoe Purcell, Greystoke Mahale channels an unrestrained castaway spirit — elegant luxury meets simple outdoorsiness, an hour's boat ride from the nearest road.

The Mahale Mountains rise to nearly 8,000 feet above Lake Tanganyika, the world's second-deepest lake, their dense sub-tropical forest home to around 1,000 chimpanzees among nine other primate species. A single habituated community, studied by Japanese researchers for over 50 years, is the focus of Greystoke's guided treks — hikes ranging from fifteen minutes to three hours, always accompanied by guides whose own families have tracked these chimps for generations. The camp's six bandas are built from reclaimed dhow wood and old fishing canoes, tucked into the treeline so as to remain nearly invisible from the water.

Experiences

Chimps, Kayaks
and the Lake

The chimpanzees are the headline draw, but Lake Tanganyika's clear, geothermal-warmed water offers an entirely different kind of adventure between treks.

Signature

Chimpanzee Trekking

Hike into the forest each morning with an experienced guide and park scout — encounters typically last up to an hour, with the clock paused as the chimps come and go.

On the Water

Kayaking

Paddle out at dawn toward the middle of the lake for coffee and sunrise, or explore the shoreline at your own pace.

Swim

Lake Tanganyika Swimming

Warmed by geothermal springs at depth, the lake stays a uniform, swimmable temperature from top to bottom.

On Foot

Forest Hikes

Hike the forest paths beyond the chimp trek in search of birds, butterflies and other shy mammals.

Waterfall

Mountain Pool Swimming

Swim in ice-cold pools at mountain waterfalls further up the slopes of the Mahale range.

Sunset

Dhow Sundowners

Cruise the shoreline on an old wooden dhow at sunset, stopping to fish along the way.

Location

The Shores of
Lake Tanganyika

Greystoke Mahale sits on a white-sand beach at the foot of the Mahale Mountains, reached only by light aircraft and boat in one of Tanzania's most remote corners.

  • Mahale Mountains National Park protects one of the last large, wild chimpanzee populations left on the continent.
  • The camp is closed annually from 17 March to the end of May, when heavy rains make forest tracks impassable.
  • Greystoke welcomes children aged 12 and over — the remote location and forest trekking make it unsuitable for younger children.
  • Chimpanzee sightings are relatively reliable year-round, though trekking conditions are most favourable in the June–October dry season.
