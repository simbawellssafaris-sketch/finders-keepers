Overview

The Spirit of
the Tang Dynasty

Founded in 1995 against the backdrop of the Mingsha Sand Dunes, the Silk Road Dunhuang Hotel resembles an ancient fortress, its large rooftops and enclosed courtyards recalling the caravanserais that once sheltered Silk Road traders.

The hotel's mud-brick walls, long winding corridors and replica Tang, Ming and Qing dynasty furniture recreate the flavour of a bygone era using natural building materials true to the northwestern Chinese tradition. Its 266 rooms open onto courtyards and gardens, with the more sought-after categories facing directly onto the dunes themselves. A rooftop terrace, the Starry Lounge, has become the hotel's real signature — a wide-open perch for watching sunrise and sunset paint the sand in shifting shades of gold, and for stargazing over one of China's darkest desert skies once night falls.

Thirty minutes from the Mogao Caves. The UNESCO-listed Mogao Grottoes, with over 500 Buddhist cave temples dating back some 1,600 years, sit roughly a 30-minute drive from the hotel — the reason most guests come to Dunhuang at all.

Experiences

Singing Dunes
& Ancient Grottoes

Dunhuang's desert setting and Buddhist heritage make it one of the most singular stops on China's ancient Silk Road.

Iconic

Mingsha Sand Dunes

Camel rides and sandboarding across the Singing Sand Dunes, named for the sound the sand makes as wind moves across its ridges.

Heritage

Mogao Caves

A UNESCO World Heritage Site holding over 500 Buddhist cave temples, their walls and ceilings covered in over a millennium of religious art.

Nearby

Crescent Lake

A slender, centuries-old spring-fed lake tucked among the dunes, a short walk from the hotel and one of Dunhuang's most photographed sights.

Evenings

Shazhou Night Market

A short drive brings guests to Dunhuang's lively night market, filled with local snacks, crafts and souvenirs.

Sunrise

Rooftop Views from the Starry Lounge

The hotel's own rooftop terrace offers uninterrupted views of sunrise and sunset over the dunes.

Geology

Yardang National Geopark

A striking desert landscape of wind-eroded rock formations, often compared to a miniature Monument Valley, a scenic drive from Dunhuang.

Location

Gateway to the
Western Silk Road

The hotel sits three kilometres south of Dunhuang city, directly on the edge of the Gobi Desert, in the Gansu province corridor that once carried Silk Road trade between China and Central Asia.

  • Located on Dunyue Road, on the edge of the Gobi Desert, three kilometres from central Dunhuang.
  • Within walking distance of the Mingsha Sand Dunes and Crescent Lake.
  • A 30-minute drive from the UNESCO-listed Mogao Caves.
  • A 15 to 20-minute drive from Dunhuang Airport, with hotel transfers available.
Fly

Dunhuang Airport connects to major Chinese cities, roughly 20 minutes from the hotel.

Rail

High-speed and overnight trains connect Dunhuang to the wider Gansu rail network, including Lanzhou and Jiayuguan.

Explore

A private guide and vehicle, arranged through the hotel concierge, is the recommended way to reach Mogao and the surrounding desert sites.
