Overview

Ocean on
Three Sides

Built on the site of the original 1905 Strand Hotel and reopened in 2015, the hotel occupies Swakopmund's historic Mole — a sea wall that shelters Namibia's most famous coastal town.

The Mole was built in 1899 by architect F.W. Ortloff, originally intended as a harbour wall; long-shore drift silted up the harbour within five years, but the small, protected beach it left behind — now Palm Beach — remains one of the best places to swim on this stretch of Atlantic coast. The Strand Hotel's 125 rooms and suites draw architecturally on that Namibian-German history, softened with contemporary design and a dramatic ocean-view atrium running the length of the building. Three sea-facing restaurants, a spa, gym and rooftop garden terrace make it Swakopmund's social hub as much as its finest address.

Experiences

Desert Meets
Atlantic

Swakopmund sits precisely where the Namib Desert reaches the sea — few towns on Earth offer such a contrast within a single day trip.

Coast

Palm Beach & The Mole

Namibia’s Atlantic runs cold, but the Mole’s sheltered sandbar makes Palm Beach one of the few genuinely swimmable stretches.

Adventure

Dune 7 & Sandboarding

Namibia's tallest accessible dune lies a short drive from town, popular for sandboarding and quad biking.

Wildlife

Catamaran Cruises

Boat trips from nearby Walvis Bay bring close encounters with Cape fur seals, dolphins, and occasionally whales.

Heritage

The Old Railway Station

Swakopmund’s 1901 railway station is now a hotel and entertainment centre, one of several colonial-era landmarks in town.

Wellness

The Atlantic Spa

Crystal steam rooms, rain showers and a garden terrace with water features, tucked inside the hotel itself.

Desert

Living Desert Tours

Half-day excursions into the dunes track the Namib's small, remarkable desert-adapted creatures — geckos, chameleons and more.

Location

On Swakopmund’s
Historic Mole

The Strand Hotel sits directly on the Mole, Swakopmund's 1899 sea wall, surrounded by the Atlantic Ocean on three sides and the Namib dunes on the fourth.

  • Swakopmund itself is a compact, walkable town — restaurants, galleries and the Swakopmund Museum are all within easy reach.
  • The Benguela Current keeps the Atlantic here cold year-round, but also draws in the marine life the coast is famous for.
  • Walvis Bay Airport is roughly a 45-minute drive, making The Strand a natural coastal stop on any Namibia circuit.
  • The hotel’s central, oceanfront position puts it within walking distance of shops, restaurants and the town’s craft markets.
Fly

Walvis Bay International Airport is roughly a 45-minute drive south of the hotel.

Drive

Swakopmund sits about 4 hours west of Windhoek by tarred road, a popular link between the capital and the coast.
