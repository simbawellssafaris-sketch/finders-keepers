OverviewA twenty-seven day odyssey across the best of Southern Africa. Itinerary

Gaborone, Botswana

Day 1 | Arrival in Gaborone

Land at Sir Seretse Khama International and transfer to the Protea by Marriott Masa Square in the centre of Botswana’s capital.

The afternoon is for acclimatising. If you would rather move, a short introduction to Gaborone takes in the Three Dikgosi Monument — a tribute to the three chiefs who travelled to London in 1895 to keep Botswana out of Rhodesian hands — and the National Museum.

Dinner is Botswana-inspired, and a gentle way in.

Protea by Marriott Gaborone Masa Square
Dinner
Okavango Delta

Day 2 | Into the Okavango

A light aircraft north into the heart of the Okavango Delta, and the flight is the first revelation — a river that never reaches the sea, spread across the Kalahari in a fan of channels, islands and floodplain, and a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Chief’s Camp sits on a remote island in the Moremi Game Reserve, on a traverse widely held to offer the finest game viewing in Southern Africa.

The first afternoon drive crosses the floodplains looking for elephant, lion, leopard, buffalo and, with luck, wild dog. Dinner is around a fire.

Chief’s Camp
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 3 | The Moremi Predator Capital

An early drive, because Moremi’s predator density is the reason to be here and the mornings are when it shows. Lion, leopard and hyena, tracked by guides whose skill is the actual product.

The plains hold huge herds of zebra, antelope and buffalo, which is what keeps the predators in business.

The afternoon is a plunge pool on your deck and then a second drive, ending with a sundowner as the sky goes.

Chief’s Camp
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 4 | A Full Day in the Delta

A morning drive, then the afternoon is yours to choose. A scenic helicopter flight gives the best possible understanding of the Delta — the network of plains, lagoons and islands only makes sense from above.

Alternatively: a massage at the camp spa, an extended drive, a photography-focused safari, or dedicated birding, since the Delta holds hundreds of resident and migrant species.

The evening is a gourmet dinner under the African sky.

Chief’s Camp
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 5 | To the Waterways of Baines’ Camp

A light aircraft to Sanctuary Baines’ Camp, on a private concession bordering Moremi and a different face of the same Delta.

The afternoon is a mokoro — a dugout canoe, poled from the stern, moving without a sound. You pass hippo and crocodile, watch elephant wading the shallows, and see the birdlife at water level. It is the classic way to experience the Delta and there is no substitute.

A sundowner out on the water closes the day.

Sanctuary Baines’ Camp
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 6 | Land, Water & Night

The full Okavango day. A guided walking safari in the morning, at ground level, reading tracks and plants and the smaller creatures a vehicle misses entirely.

A motorised safari boat in the afternoon, through the wider channels at a pace a mokoro cannot manage.

Then a night drive with spotlights. The Delta after dark is a different place: leopard, civet, genet, bushbaby and nocturnal birds.

Sanctuary Baines’ Camp
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 7 | A Balloon Over the Delta

A sunrise hot-air balloon over the Delta, subject to season, weather and availability. Floating silently above it as it wakes — the channels catching first light, herds moving across the floodplain — is the finale the Botswana chapter deserves.

A celebratory breakfast on landing, then an afternoon of doing very little: the pool, the spa, or the deck.

A private Okavango farewell sundowner and dinner.

Sanctuary Baines’ Camp
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner
Harare, Zimbabwe

Day 8 | Botswana → Zimbabwe

A flight to Harare and a transfer to the Monomotapa Hotel.

The afternoon is for recovering from the Botswana sector. If you have the energy, a short city experience takes in the National Heroes Acre, Harare Gardens or the craft markets.

Dinner is Zimbabwean.

Monomotapa Hotel
Breakfast, Dinner
Victoria Falls

Day 9 | Into the Zambezi Wilderness

A flight to Victoria Falls and a transfer to Matetsi, set within a 136,000-acre private reserve with nine miles of its own Zambezi frontage.

The afternoon drive works the Matetsi reserve, which holds enormous herds of elephant and buffalo alongside lion, leopard, wild dog and hyena.

At sunset you board a boat on the private stretch of river. Elephant and hippo on the bank, the Zambezi going gold, and no other vessel in sight — the private frontage is the whole point.

Matetsi Victoria Falls
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 10 | The Smoke That Thunders

A morning drive through the private reserve and a guided walk for the smaller detail.

The afternoon is Victoria Falls itself. Walking the viewpoints you feel the spray from a long way back — Mosi-oa-Tunya, the Smoke that Thunders, is the largest single sheet of falling water on Earth, and the noise is the part photographs cannot carry.

Optional additions include a helicopter flight over the falls, which gives the scale, and cultural experiences in the town. Back to Matetsi for dinner.

Matetsi Victoria Falls
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner
South Luangwa, Zambia

Day 11 | Into the Valley of the Leopard

A flight into Zambia and the South Luangwa National Park, one of the great wilderness areas in Africa and the place walking safaris were invented.

Puku Ridge Camp sits in a productive part of the park and runs both vehicle and walking safaris.

The first drive introduces a park renowned above all for leopard. The plains, woodland and river support lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo, hyena and wild dog.

Puku Ridge Camp
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 12 | Walking, and a Night Safari

The day South Luangwa is famous for. A guided walking safari with an expert who teaches you to read the bush — tracks, droppings, plants and bird calls, and the way the whole system fits together. Walking is where the small things finally register.

In the evening, a night drive. With a spotlight the park changes personnel entirely: leopard, hyena, African wild cat and everything else that waits for dark.

Puku Ridge Camp
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 13 | Leopard Country

A dawn drive, then an afternoon built around photography. South Luangwa’s leopard concentrations are genuinely exceptional and your guide will work to get you the light and the angle rather than just the sighting.

A private bush dinner under the sky closes the South Luangwa chapter.

Puku Ridge Camp
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner
Lower Zambezi

Day 14 | To the Lower Zambezi

A flight to the Lower Zambezi National Park — a completely different safari environment, laid along the bank of the river with the Zambian escarpment behind.

Royal Zambezi Lodge is intimate and family-owned, with three kilometres of unspoilt river frontage.

The afternoon is a river safari, then a Zambezi sundowner cruise: elephant and hippo on the shoreline and the sun going down over the water.

Royal Zambezi Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 15 | By Land and Water

A canoe safari in the morning, which is the most intimate way to see this river — low, quiet, and closer to hippo than most people expect to get.

A game drive in the national park in the afternoon. The lodge also runs walking safaris, fishing and birding, and the evening is yours to choose from: another walk, a cultural visit, or a private bush dinner.

Royal Zambezi Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner
Windhoek, Namibia

Day 16 | Zambia → Namibia

A flight to Windhoek and a transfer to the Hilton.

The afternoon explores the centre: the Independence Memorial Museum, the historic Christ Church, and the Namibia Craft Centre for genuinely good local work.

Dinner is Namibian, which is a stranger and better cuisine than most visitors expect.

Hilton Windhoek
Breakfast, Dinner
Etosha National Park

Day 17 | Into Etosha

North to Etosha and Mokuti, which sits forty metres from the Von Lindequist Gate at the park’s eastern edge.

The first Etosha drive follows. The park is built around a salt pan visible from space, and the savannah around it supports a remarkable concentration of animals drawn to the waterholes — which is how game viewing works here. You do not find the animals; you sit at the water and they come.

Mokuti Etosha
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 18 | Waterholes & a Night Drive

An early drive deep into the park, spending real time at the waterholes — Okaukuejo in particular — watching the sequence of who drinks, who waits and who does not dare.

The afternoon returns towards Mokuti, and the evening is a night game drive for hyena, jackal, honey badger and porcupine.

Mokuti Etosha
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 19 | Culture, Birds & Namutoni

The final Etosha day goes beyond the drive. Birding and a guided bush walk in the morning, reading the smaller details of a desert-edge ecosystem.

The cultural component is a visit to an Oshiwambo community, arranged as a genuine exchange about daily life rather than a staged event.

The afternoon visits Namutoni, the old German fort inside the park, and the evening is a farewell dinner at the lodge.

Mokuti Etosha
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner
Cape Town, South Africa

Day 20 | Namibia → Cape Town

A flight to Cape Town and a transfer to The Silo, built into the elevator tower of the 1924 grain silo above the Zeitz MOCAA museum at the V&A Waterfront.

The afternoon is the Waterfront and, if you want it, the museum below your feet — the largest collection of contemporary African art on the continent.

Dinner is fine dining, in a city that does it as well as anywhere.

The Silo Hotel
Breakfast, Dinner
Sabi Sand

Day 21 | The Final Safari Begins

A flight northeast to the Sabi Sand and a transfer to Lion Sands River Lodge, on the bank of the perennial Sabie River, the only private access to it in the reserve.

The Sabi Sand is known globally for big-cat density and above all for leopard, which here are habituated enough that sightings run long and close rather than fleeting.

The first drive, and a sundowner in the bush.

Lion Sands River Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 22 | Big Five Country

An early drive with a guide and a tracker working as a pair, which is the arrangement that makes Sabi Sand sightings what they are. All of the Big Five are here and the trackers are extremely good at the elusive ones.

The afternoon is the pool or the Amani Spa, and the evening is a second drive followed by an outdoor bush dinner.

Lion Sands River Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 23 | Walking Safari & Wellness

A guided walking safari in the morning, at ground level with a trained guide, learning to read the ecosystem rather than simply look at it.

The afternoon is the Amani Spa, with treatments drawn from African traditions.

The evening drive is a night safari, and the reserve after dark is a different reserve.

Lion Sands River Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 24 | The Final Big Five Day

The last full safari day, and a dawn drive dedicated to whatever is still outstanding — lion, leopard, elephant, rhino or buffalo.

A relaxed afternoon at the lodge, then a grand farewell: a private sundowner followed by a bush dinner, and the end of twenty-four days of African wilderness.

Lion Sands River Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner
Plettenberg Bay

Day 25 | Sabi Sand → the Garden Route

A flight back to Cape Town, where your private driver meets you for the scenic road journey along the Garden Route to Plettenberg Bay.

Check in at The Plettenberg, a five-star property on a rocky headland above the Indian Ocean.

The afternoon is the beach — Lookout Beach, or the Robberg peninsula — and dinner is oceanfront.

The Plettenberg
Breakfast, Dinner

Day 26 | Whales, Beaches & Farewell

A whale-watching excursion in the morning, when the season allows. Southern Right whales arrive from around June and stay through November, with the peak in August and September, and dolphins and seals are present year-round.

The afternoon is the beach or the town, and then the drive back to Cape Town and a final night at The Silo, with Table Mountain in the window.

The Silo Hotel
Breakfast, Dinner

Day 27 | Departure

Breakfast at The Silo and, depending on your flight, a final morning at the V&A Waterfront. Your private driver takes you to Cape Town International Airport.

Travel Kit

Soft duffel, sun hat and binoculars

  • What is inside it — A wide-brim sun hat, a pair of compact binoculars, a dry bag and a neutral-toned buff for dust.
  • Yours to keep — The bag and everything in it is a gift, not a loan.
  • Storage for the rest — Anything over the limit is stored securely at your first city hotel and returned to you at the end.

Note

Light aircraft enforce a 15 kg soft-bag limit

Every camp-to-camp flight on this route runs on light aircraft with a strict 15 kg soft-luggage allowance and no hard cases. The Okavango balloon depends on season, weather and availability, and the Mount-of-the-Delta flooding varies year to year — the Delta is at its highest between roughly May and August, months after the rain that caused it. Whale watching off Plettenberg Bay runs from June to November, with the peak in August and September.

Documents

Multiple visas and a Yellow Fever certificate

  • Passport — Valid for at least six months beyond your return date, with at least two blank pages per country visited.

  • Yellow Fever — A Yellow Fever certificate is mandatory on this itinerary, and is checked at borders. It must be issued at least ten days before travel.
  • Health — Routine vaccinations should be current, and malaria prophylaxis is recommended across the route. Speak to your doctor at least six weeks before departure.
  • We handle the paperwork — We complete and submit your visa, eVisa or travel-authorisation applications on your behalf. You supply the documents once; we do the filing, track the approval and hold the confirmations for you.
