Itinerary

Gaborone

Day 1 | Arrival in Gaborone — Welcome to Botswana

Your Botswana adventure begins the moment your aircraft touches down at Sir Seretse Khama International Airport. A {{brand}} representative greets you in the arrivals hall and escorts you into a waiting luxury transfer vehicle. The drive carries you through Gaborone's leafy suburbs, past modern buildings and bustling markets — a gentle introduction to Botswana's famous hospitality. At the Protea by Marriott Hotel you are checked in immediately, no waiting. The remainder of the day is unscheduled — rest, explore the hotel's facilities or swim in the pool. As evening approaches, a welcome dinner is served at the hotel's signature restaurant, where your guide joins you to brief the journey ahead over maps and stories of the bush. Tomorrow, the wilderness begins.

Mashatu Reserve

Day 2 | Charter Flight to Mashatu Reserve — The Land of the Giants

After a leisurely breakfast, transfer to Gaborone Airport for your private charter flight to Mashatu Reserve (approximately 1 hour 15 minutes). From above, the outskirts of Gaborone give way to open savannah, ancient riverbeds and baobab-dotted plains. You may spot wildlife from the air. Upon landing, your guide is waiting. The drive to Mashatu Main Lodge takes you through the reserve's northern sector — within minutes, a herd of elephants with tusks curving toward the earth, a family of giraffes at the highest acacia leaves, a troop of baboons crossing ahead. You arrive to cold towels and a welcome drink. Check into your suite — a private deck overlooking the bush, a king-sized bed in fine linens, an outdoor shower open to the African night. Lunch, a rest, then sundowners on the main deck as elephants silhouette against the fading sky. Dinner by candlelight. The African night surrounds you.

Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 3 | Mashatu Reserve — The Giants of the North

The day begins before sunrise. By 6:00 AM, you are in your private safari vehicle as the morning air turns cool and golden. Within the first hour, a herd of more than fifty elephants — a massive bull tearing bark from a baobab, mothers guarding calves, adolescents chasing each other through dust clouds. Your guide explains elephant social structure, their memory, their grief. Mid-morning: a table set in a clearing with white linen, silverware and china cups. A chef prepares a full breakfast over an open fire while zebra graze a hundred metres away. Return to the lodge for lunch and a siesta. At 4:00 PM, reports of a leopard near the Majale River. You drive toward the acacia trees — and there she is, draped across a branch, an impala carcass wedged above her, watching you with half-closed eyes. A sundowner under the first stars. Bonfire dinner. Somewhere in the darkness, an elephant rumbles.

Day 4 | Mashatu Reserve — Matebole Underground Photo Hide

Today you visit the world-famous Matebole Underground Photo Hide — sunk into the ground at a waterhole, bringing you eye-level with wildlife as they arrive to drink. Large ground-level openings allow unobstructed views and extraordinary low-angle photography. Your guide is an expert photographer, available to help with settings and composition. Warthogs, impala and then elephants arrive in succession — seeing them from underground, at eye level, is an experience unlike any other. A young elephant approaches the hide, curious, and you photograph it from just a few metres away. The session lasts approximately three hours. You emerge exhilarated, memory cards full. The afternoon is yours to rest, swim and review your images. An evening game drive closes the day with a lion pride in the shade of an acacia and a sundowner overlooking the river.

Day 5 | Mashatu Reserve — Horseback Wilderness Safari

Today you experience Mashatu from an entirely different perspective — on horseback. After an early breakfast, your equestrian guide assesses your riding experience and matches you with the appropriate horse. Mashatu's open terrain is perfect for riding, and the silence of hooves on sand allows you to approach wildlife more closely than any vehicle. You ride out as the sun rises over the baobabs — through open grasslands, acacia woodlands, ancient riverbeds and baobab groves. Within the first hour, a herd of zebra watch you curiously and return to grazing. Your horse is calm, untroubled. You feel a connection to the landscape that is impossible from a vehicle. The ride lasts approximately three hours with a stop for bush refreshments. Return to the lodge exhilarated and deeply satisfied. Evening game drive, sundowner, bonfire dinner.

Day 6 | Mashatu Reserve — Full Day Safari & Bush Breakfast

A 5:30 AM wake-up call. A full day dedicated entirely to Mashatu's extraordinary wildlife. Reports of a cheetah with cubs near the Limpopo River — within an hour you find them on a rocky outcrop, cubs tumbling over each other, the mother watching with patience and love. After the cheetahs, your guide takes you off the main tracks into the reserve's most remote corners. A pride of lions resting in the shade of a baobab. A herd of buffalo — massive, ancient, watchful. A pair of rhinos, rare and magnificent. A large python coiled in the branches of a fig tree. Mid-morning: a bush breakfast set under an acacia. Return for lunch and a long siesta. At 4:00 PM, out again. The elephants gather to drink — dozens of them. Sundowner. The sky turns violet, then deep indigo.

Chobe National Park

Day 7 | Mashatu → Chobe National Park — Flight of the Elephants

After breakfast, bid farewell to Mashatu and transfer to the airstrip for your charter flight to Chobe National Park (approximately 1 hour 30 minutes). From above, the baobab-dotted plains give way to lush riverine forests and winding waterways. The Chobe River glints in the morning light, and elephants are visible from the air — hundreds of them gathering along the riverbanks. Your new guide is waiting on landing. The drive to Chobe Game Lodge reveals your first animals immediately — more elephants than you have ever seen in one place. Hippos grunt in the river. Crocodiles bask on sandbanks. Check into your luxurious river-facing suite — balcony, deep soaking tub, air conditioning. Lunch on the main deck. At 4:00 PM, a first Chobe game drive: a herd of more than one hundred elephants drinking, bathing and playing in the river. Sundowner. Dinner. The sounds of the African night surround you.

Day 8 | Chobe — Private River Safari & The Giants of the Water

Today you explore Chobe from the water. After breakfast, board a private river safari boat. From the water, hippos surface beside the boat, crocodiles slide off the banks, and elephants wade in to drink and bathe. Your guide navigates close to a pod of thirty or more hippos, resting in the shallows. The birdlife is extraordinary — African fish eagles, saddle-billed storks, goliath herons, kingfishers of every colour. Mid-morning: a bush breakfast on a sandbank, a table with white linen and silver, a chef preparing eggs while hippos surface behind you. Return to the lodge for lunch and a siesta. An evening game drive discovers a pride of twelve lions resting in the shade of an acacia. Sundowner over the floodplains as hundreds of elephants gather to drink. Bonfire on the riverbank.

Day 9 | Chobe — Full Day Safari & Bush Lunch

A full day dedicated to Chobe's extraordinary wildlife. Reports of a leopard near the Nogatsaa area — elusive in Chobe, but today luck is with you. A female leopard rests on an acacia branch, an impala carcass wedged in the fork above her, watching you with calm intelligent eyes. Thirty minutes alone with her, no other vehicles. After the leopard, the guide takes you off the main tracks: buffalo, rhinos, wild dogs resting in the shade. Mid-morning bush breakfast. Lunch served in the bush — a gourmet picnic at a viewpoint over the floodplains. The afternoon continues deeper into Chobe's remote corners. Sundowner over the river as the elephants mass to drink. Return to the lodge for dinner. Bonfire on the riverbank.

Day 10 | Chobe — Hot Air Balloon Safari

The alarm comes at 4:00 AM. A short drive to the launch site on the edge of the Chobe floodplains. The balloon inflates in the darkness, burners casting an orange glow. You climb into the basket. The ground falls away. The Chobe River winds below you, glittering in the first light. Elephants gather along the banks in their hundreds, moving in single file, shadows stretching across the floodplains. Hippos leave the river. Buffalo move across the grasslands. Lions return from their night hunts. The sun breaks over the horizon. The floodplains turn gold, then amber. The balloon drifts for over an hour. You descend to a champagne breakfast on the floodplains — white linen flapping in the morning breeze. A morning game drive follows. This evening, a private bush dinner on the Chobe riverbank: lanterns in the acacia branches, white linen, bonfire crackling, elephants watching from the opposite bank.

Day 11 | Chobe — Walking Safari & River Cruise

A walking safari along the Chobe River with an armed ranger. Walking through the bush is a fundamentally different experience — sounds, smells, textures all amplified. Your guide reads the tracks: an elephant passed here last night, a lion rested under this tree, a crocodile slid into the river at dawn. You walk for two hours, stopping often to observe and absorb. Return to the lodge for a late breakfast. The afternoon brings a private river safari boat for one final exploration of the Chobe River — the light golden, hippos surfacing, crocodiles on the banks, an elephant matriarch leading her calf across the river. A sundowner on a sandbank in the middle of the river as the sky turns violet. Dinner. Bonfire on the riverbank.

Victoria Falls, Zimbabwe

Day 13 | Victoria Falls — The Smoke That Thunders

After an early breakfast, depart Chobe Game Lodge at first light for one of the journey's most anticipated excursions. Your guide drives you to the Kazungula Border Post, clearing customs and immigration (all visa fees included). The journey from Chobe to Victoria Falls takes approximately 1.5–2 hours. You hear it before you see it — a deep, continuous rumble the Kololo people called "Mosi-oa-Tunya": the Smoke That Thunders. Then you see the spray rising hundreds of metres, visible from miles away, rainbows dancing across the gorge. Your guide leads you through the network of paths following the gorge edge — the Devil's Cataract, Main Falls, Rainbow Falls, Horseshoe Falls, the Eastern Cataract. Each viewpoint offers a different perspective on this extraordinary place. David Livingstone was the first European to witness it in 1855, but the local people had known its power for centuries. Lunch at a carefully selected restaurant overlooking the Zambezi. Afternoon free to explore the craft markets or take tea at the historic Victoria Falls Hotel. Return to Chobe at sunset.

Journey Details

Minimum Age

Varies by camp, activity and national park

  • Camps and lodges — Most safari camps accept children from six years, with shared game-drive vehicles usually from eight to twelve. Many properties will take younger children on a privately booked vehicle.
  • Walking safaris — Guided walking safaris carry a firm minimum of fifteen or sixteen years across most reserves. This is set by the licensing authority, not the camp, and is never waived.
  • Gorilla and chimpanzee trekking — Uganda and Rwanda both enforce a strict minimum of fifteen years. It is a national park regulation and there are no exceptions.
  • Hot-air balloon safaris — Operators generally require children to be at least seven, and tall enough to brace unaided in the basket on landing.
  • Family and exclusive-use camps — A number of our camps run dedicated family units, child-minding and tailored activity programmes, and will relax their usual age limit for a sole-use booking.
  • Talk to us first — Age limits vary from property to property and from activity to activity. Speak to our experts before you book, and we will pre-arrange the details with each lodge around the ages of the children travelling with you.

Safari Duffel

Duffel, safari hat, vest and hooded jumper

  • Your duffel — A soft-sided {{brand}} duffel, sized for the 15 kg limit on the light aircraft that move you between camps. Rigid suitcases cannot be carried on those flights at all.
  • What is inside it — A branded {{brand}} safari hat, a safari vest, and a hooded jumper for cold dawn starts and balloon mornings.
  • Yours to keep — The bag and everything in it is a gift, not a loan. Nothing is returned at the end of your journey.
  • Packing around it — Soft luggage, neutral colours, and layers. Laundry is included at nearly every camp, which is why 15 kg goes further than it sounds.

Note

Water levels shape the Okavango Delta experience

The Delta's flood waters typically peak between June and August, affecting which activities (mokoro, boating, or 4x4 game drives) are available at each camp. Malaria prophylaxis is recommended.

  • Early starts — Safaris begin very early in the morning. Your guide will confirm what time you will be collected from your camp or lodge.
  • Tailoring this journey — If you would like this package with more or fewer days, arranged for a group, a family or a solo traveller, or with add-ons, reach out and we will tailor it for you.
  • Route changes — Your guide or driver may alter the route in certain circumstances, but every destination set out in the itinerary will be covered.

Documents

Visa-free for most nationalities

  • Passport — Valid for at least six months beyond your arrival date, with at least two blank pages for stamps.
  • Visa — Visa-free for up to 90 days for UK, EU, US, Canadian, Australian and most Commonwealth and SADC passport holders. Other nationalities apply through the Botswana eVisa portal before travel.
  • Travelling with children — An unabridged birth certificate showing both parents is required for every traveller under 18. Where a child travels with one parent only, or with neither, a sworn parental consent affidavit is also needed.
  • Light-aircraft weight declaration — Camp-to-camp flights in the Delta require the actual body weight of every passenger in advance. It is a safety calculation for small aircraft, not a formality, and we must submit it with your booking.
  • We file it for you — Whether you are flying in from overseas or travelling from elsewhere on the continent, we complete and submit your visa application on your behalf. You hand us your documents once. We do the filing, chase the approval, and carry the confirmations in your travel pack so nothing rests on you remembering a reference number at a counter.
  • Most of Africa is open to you — The great majority of our African destinations are now either visa-free or issue a visa on arrival, so an embassy queue is rarely in your future at all. Where a visa on arrival does apply, we prepare and lodge the paperwork ahead of you — you reach the desk with a finished file rather than a blank form and a biro.
  • Before you travel — Entry rules change frequently and without much notice. Your specialist confirms the current requirements for every passport in your party at the time of booking, and checks them again before you fly.

Guaranteed Departures

Private, tailor-made departures on your dates, or join a small group

  • Once confirmed, you travel — When your departure date is confirmed, it is guaranteed. We do not cancel, merge or reschedule a confirmed departure for commercial reasons, however the final numbers fall.
  • What confirms a departure — A departure becomes confirmed once the agreed number of travellers has been reached and paid for in line with the agreed payment schedule. Places held without payment do not count towards it.
  • If the minimum is not reached — Where a group departure has not reached its agreed numbers, we tell you early rather than late, and you choose: move to the next available date, continue at a revised small-group rate, or take a full refund of everything you have paid.
  • Solo journeys — Solo and tailor-made itineraries carry no group minimum at all. Your dates are confirmed the moment your deposit is received.
  • Visas — We prepare and submit your visa application on your behalf. Please note that the granting of a visa rests solely with the immigration authority of the country concerned, and is never guaranteed by us.
  • Medication — We arrange malaria prophylaxis, together with anti-nausea and anti-anxiety tablets, for your journey. Any prescription medicine should be confirmed with your own doctor before you travel — they remain the final authority on what is suitable for you.
  • Africa is not always hot — Despite the stereotype, Africa moves through distinct seasons and mornings at altitude can be genuinely cold. A hooded jumper is provided for cold conditions, and for every guest joining a hot-air balloon safari.
  • Dietary requirements — If you have strict dietary needs, please tell us at the time of booking. This includes vegans, guests in recovery, non-drinkers, and anyone following a prescription from their doctor.
  • Travel insurance — We facilitate travel insurance for our clients. If you are already covered, please say so during booking so we can take it into account in your final quote.
  • Tailoring your quote — Some of our inclusions — hot-air balloon safaris, for example — may not be what you want. Point these out and we will adjust your final offer accordingly.
  • The Maasai massage & spa recovery — Guests travelling the safari circuit by road rather than by air should expect long and tiring days. The rough terrain of the jungle gives the vehicle a constant jolting ride, known affectionately as the Maasai massage. A spa session is provided at the end of your safari to recover.
  • What to pack — Neutral colours are the rule on safari: khaki, olive, stone and brown. Avoid black and dark blue, which draw tsetse flies, and leave camouflage at home entirely — it is reserved for the military and illegal for civilians in several African countries. Bring layers for cold dawn game drives, long sleeves and trousers for dusk when the mosquitoes are out, closed walking shoes, a sun hat and sunglasses. Lodge dinners are smart-casual; nothing formal is needed.
  • Your safari duffel & kit — The duffel bag is used when flying into the parks, where luggage is limited to 15 kg per person. A branded {{brand}} safari hat and safari vest are also provided.
