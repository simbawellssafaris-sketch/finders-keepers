OverviewThis is not a safari for the casual traveller. This is a journey for those who have already seen the Serengeti — and who now seek something deeper, wilder, more remote. Over fourteen extraordinary days, The Untamed Sanctuaries moves through three of Tanzania's least-visited wilderness areas: Ruaha National Park, where predator densities rival the Serengeti but crowds do not; Nyerere National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site where walking safaris and boat safaris reveal the African bush from entirely different perspectives; and Mahale Mountains National Park, where habituated chimpanzees live in the wild forests cascading down to the turquoise shores of Lake Tanganyika. Three extraordinary lodges. Every detail anticipated. Every meal, drink, flight, activity and photograph included in one single, transparent price. No hidden costs. No surprises. Only the journey.

Itinerary

Dar es Salaam

Day 1 | Arrival in Dar es Salaam — Welcome to Tanzania

Your Tanzanian adventure begins the moment your aircraft touches down at Julius Nyerere International Airport. A {{brand}} representative greets you in the arrivals hall — no searching for signs, no waiting in long queues. You are escorted through VIP formalities and into a waiting luxury transfer vehicle for the journey to the Element by Marriott Hotel.

For arrivals between 10:00 AM and 3:00 PM, a complimentary private city tour awaits — the National Museum of Tanzania (home to the famous Olduvai Gorge hominid fossils), the Village Museum, the Kivukoni Fish Market, the Askari Monument, and the St. Joseph's Cathedral. As evening approaches, a welcome dinner celebrates Tanzanian cuisine: grilled Indian Ocean seafood, coconut-infused curries, and fresh tropical fruit. Your guide joins you to brief you on the days ahead.

Ruaha National Park

Day 2 | Charter Flight to Ruaha — Arrival at Jabali Ridge

After breakfast, check out and transfer to Julius Nyerere International Airport for your private charter flight to Ruaha National Park (approximately 2 hours). From above, baobab-dotted hills and open savannah stretch to the horizon. Wildlife is visible from the air.

On landing, your guide and vehicle are waiting. The drive to Jabali Ridge becomes your first Ruaha game drive immediately — elephants with curving tusks, a resting lion pride, giraffes at the acacia canopy. Jabali Ridge appears on a rocky kopje, its eight suites designed to blend into the landscape. After lunch on the main deck, a 4 PM game drive reveals a pride of twenty-two lions on the banks of the Great Ruaha River. A sundowner overlooks the river at sunset, followed by a bonfire dinner on the main deck as lions roar in the distance.

Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner

Day 3 | Ruaha National Park — The Predator Capital

Dawn game drive from 6 AM — Ruaha's predator densities are extraordinary. Within the first hour, a pack of twelve African wild dogs rests in the shade of a baobab after a morning hunt: brown, black and white coats, each individual unique. Your guide has tracked this pack for years and knows each animal by name.

A full bush breakfast is prepared on white linen beneath an acacia tree as zebra graze a hundred metres away. After a lodge siesta, the afternoon drive finds a female leopard draped across a branch above a wedged impala carcass — watched for twenty minutes with no other vehicles in sight. Sundowner overlooking the river, then a bonfire dinner as the fire crackles and a hyena calls across the plains. Throughout the day, your guide captures photographs of you: your laughter, your wonder, your joy.

Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner

Day 4 | Ruaha National Park — Walking Safari

Today you experience Ruaha on foot. Accompanied by an armed ranger and your guide, you set out across the park. The sounds, smells and textures of the bush are amplified entirely. Your guide reads the tracks: an elephant passed here last night. A lion rested under this tree. A leopard dragged its kill across this path.

He points out medicinal plants used by local communities for generations and explains the relationship between trees, soil, water and the animals that depend on them. Two hours of walking, stopping often to observe and absorb. A bush breakfast is served under a large baobab — white linen, silverware, eggs prepared to order as zebra graze nearby. An afternoon game drive closes with a sundowner as elephant families gather at the Great Ruaha River.

Day 5 | Ruaha National Park — Hot Air Balloon Safari

4:00 AM. The balloon inflates in darkness, burners casting an orange glow across the savannah. You climb into the basket. The ground falls away. The Great Ruaha River winds below, glittering in first light. Elephant families move in single file along the banks. Lions return from night hunts. The sun breaks the horizon and the plains turn gold. An hour above Ruaha as only a bird sees it.

A champagne bush breakfast awaits on the plains — white linen flapping in the morning breeze. After breakfast, the morning drive finds a mother cheetah with three cubs on a rocky outcrop, the cubs tumbling and chasing each other in circles. A gourmet picnic lunch at a river viewpoint is followed by a final afternoon game drive, sundowner and bonfire dinner.

Nyerere National Park

Day 6 | Charter Flight to Nyerere — Arrival at Sand River Selous

After breakfast, farewell to Jabali Ridge and a charter flight to Nyerere National Park (approximately 1 hour 15 minutes). The baobab hills of Ruaha give way to dense woodland and the winding waterways of the Rufiji below.

Your new guide meets you at the airstrip. Sand River Selous — an elegant tented camp on the banks of the Rufiji River — becomes home for three nights. An afternoon game drive reveals over a hundred elephants drinking and bathing in the river, a young calf practising with its trunk. Sundowner at the riverbank as the sky turns violet.

Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner

Day 7 | Nyerere National Park — Boat Safari on the Rufiji River

After breakfast, you board a private boat and set out along the Rufiji River. Hippos surface beside the vessel, their enormous yawns revealing teeth the size of a man's fist. Crocodiles slide off sandbanks. Elephants wade into the water to drink, cross and bathe. A pod of thirty or more hippos rests in the shallows. African fish eagles, saddle-billed storks, goliath herons and kingfishers of every colour line the banks.

A bush breakfast is served on a mid-river sandbank — table, umbrella, eggs to order, hippos surfacing behind you. After a lodge siesta, an evening boat safari watches an elephant family crossing the river, the matriarch leading as her calf swims close beside her. A sundowner is served on a sandbank in the middle of the river.

Day 8 | Nyerere National Park — Walking Safari

One of the few places in Tanzania where walking safaris are permitted, Nyerere reveals itself entirely on foot. Accompanied by an armed ranger, you walk inland through open woodland and grassland, your guide reading the tracks — an elephant, a lion at rest, a crocodile entering the river at dawn.

A bush breakfast is laid in the shade of an acacia tree. After the lodge siesta, an evening game drive finds a lion pride in the shade — a sundowner overlooking the river as the plains settle into evening. Bonfire lit on the riverbank.

Mahale Mountains National Park

Day 9 | Charter Flight to Mahale — Arrival at Greystoke

After breakfast, farewell to Sand River Selous and a charter flight to Mahale Mountains National Park (approximately 2 hours, with a brief refuelling stop). The woodlands of Nyerere give way to the dramatic peaks of the Mahale Mountains rising steeply from Lake Tanganyika.

On landing at the Mahale airstrip, a boat waits to take you across the water to Greystoke Mahale — a collection of driftwood and palm-thatch bandas scattered along a white-sand beach. The staff sings a welcome song. Your banda is private and romantic, with a king-sized bed, a bucket-shower bathroom and a veranda overlooking the world's longest freshwater lake. Dinner and a bonfire on the beach by lantern light.

Greystoke Mahale, Mahale Mountains National Park →
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner

Day 10 | Mahale Mountains — Chimpanzee Tracking

5:30 AM. After a briefing, you set out into the Mahale forest with your guide. The mountains are steep and the forest dense. After an hour of walking, he stops — he has heard a pant-hoot rising in pitch. You move quickly but quietly, and then you see them: a family of chimpanzees feeding in a fig tree. A mother with an infant on her back. A large dominant male watching you with intelligent eyes. Juveniles chasing each other through the branches.

One hour with the chimpanzees — the time passes too quickly. Your guide explains their behaviour, family dynamics and individual relationships, knowing each by name. The walk back through the forest is quiet and reflective. An afternoon lake cruise at 4 PM ends with a sundowner on a sandbank in the middle of Lake Tanganyika.

Greystoke Mahale, Mahale Mountains National Park →
Breakfast, Lunch & Dinner

Day 11 | Mahale Mountains — Second Chimpanzee Tracking

The chimpanzees have moved during the night. You set out in a different direction, climbing higher into the mountains. After two hours, you find them again — today on the ground. A mother grooms her infant. A male displays, beating his chest and charging through the undergrowth. Juveniles chase each other in circles. Another hour passes too quickly.

Today your guide captures photographs of you with the chimpanzees — rare and precious images. The afternoon is free for lake swimming, beach walking, or a guided nature walk through the forest. A beach bonfire closes the evening.

Day 12 | Mahale Mountains — Forest Walk & Lake Relaxation

Today is intentionally unscheduled — a day of rest and reflection in one of Africa's most beautiful settings. Options include a guided forest walk for colobus monkeys and birds, swimming in Lake Tanganyika, kayaking along the shoreline, a visit to the nearby Tongwe village, or simply resting in your banda listening to the sounds of the forest and the lake.

A final lake cruise at 4 PM ends with a sundowner on a sandbank as the sky turns violet. A special farewell dinner is prepared by the chef — stories of chimpanzees and mountains shared around the beach bonfire.

Your final full day at Greystoke Mahale. You may choose one last optional chimpanzee tracking excursion (additional permit fee applies), a guided forest walk, swimming in the lake, kayaking, or simply resting on the beach. Breakfast and lunch are served on the sand.

A farewell sundowner is served on the beach as the sun drops behind the Mahale peaks. Dinner by lantern light. A final bonfire on the sand as the stars of Lake Tanganyika fill the sky above.

Journey Details

Minimum Age

Varies by camp, activity and national park

  • Camps and lodges — Most safari camps accept children from six years, with shared game-drive vehicles usually from eight to twelve. Many properties will take younger children on a privately booked vehicle.
  • Walking safaris — Guided walking safaris carry a firm minimum of fifteen or sixteen years across most reserves. This is set by the licensing authority, not the camp, and is never waived.
  • Gorilla and chimpanzee trekking — Uganda and Rwanda both enforce a strict minimum of fifteen years. It is a national park regulation and there are no exceptions.
  • Hot-air balloon safaris — Operators generally require children to be at least seven, and tall enough to brace unaided in the basket on landing.
  • Family and exclusive-use camps — A number of our camps run dedicated family units, child-minding and tailored activity programmes, and will relax their usual age limit for a sole-use booking.
  • Talk to us first — Age limits vary from property to property and from activity to activity. Speak to our experts before you book, and we will pre-arrange the details with each lodge around the ages of the children travelling with you.

Safari Duffel

Duffel, safari hat, vest and hooded jumper

  • Your duffel — A soft-sided {{brand}} duffel, sized for the 15 kg limit on the light aircraft that move you between camps. Rigid suitcases cannot be carried on those flights at all.
  • What is inside it — A branded {{brand}} safari hat, a safari vest, and a hooded jumper for cold dawn starts and balloon mornings.
  • Yours to keep — The bag and everything in it is a gift, not a loan. Nothing is returned at the end of your journey.
  • Packing around it — Soft luggage, neutral colours, and layers. Laundry is included at nearly every camp, which is why 15 kg goes further than it sounds.

Note

Migration timing varies with seasonal rains

The wildebeest migration moves across the Serengeti ecosystem on a roughly annual cycle driven by rainfall, so exact river-crossing locations shift year to year. Malaria prophylaxis is recommended for all safari areas.

  • Early starts — Safaris begin very early in the morning. Your guide will confirm what time you will be collected from your camp or lodge.
  • Tailoring this journey — If you would like this package with more or fewer days, arranged for a group, a family or a solo traveller, or with add-ons, reach out and we will tailor it for you.
  • Route changes — Your guide or driver may alter the route in certain circumstances, but every destination set out in the itinerary will be covered.

Documents

Visa on arrival or e-Visa, from $50

  • Passport — Valid for at least six months beyond your arrival date, with at least two blank pages for stamps.
  • Visa — Apply for the eVisa online (one to three working days) or take a visa on arrival at the main airports and land borders. Single entry is US$50. US passport holders are issued the US$100 multiple-entry visa instead.
  • One visa, both halves — A single Tanzanian visa covers the mainland safari circuit and the Zanzibar archipelago. You do not need a second visa to fly to the islands.
  • Yellow fever — A vaccination certificate is required if you are arriving from, or have transited, a country with yellow fever risk.
  • We file it for you — Whether you are flying in from overseas or travelling from elsewhere on the continent, we complete and submit your visa application on your behalf. You hand us your documents once. We do the filing, chase the approval, and carry the confirmations in your travel pack so nothing rests on you remembering a reference number at a counter.
  • Most of Africa is open to you — The great majority of our African destinations are now either visa-free or issue a visa on arrival, so an embassy queue is rarely in your future at all. Where a visa on arrival does apply, we prepare and lodge the paperwork ahead of you — you reach the desk with a finished file rather than a blank form and a biro.
  • Before you travel — Entry rules change frequently and without much notice. Your specialist confirms the current requirements for every passport in your party at the time of booking, and checks them again before you fly.

Guaranteed Departures

Private, tailor-made departures on your dates, or join a small group

  • Once confirmed, you travel — When your departure date is confirmed, it is guaranteed. We do not cancel, merge or reschedule a confirmed departure for commercial reasons, however the final numbers fall.
  • What confirms a departure — A departure becomes confirmed once the agreed number of travellers has been reached and paid for in line with the agreed payment schedule. Places held without payment do not count towards it.
  • If the minimum is not reached — Where a group departure has not reached its agreed numbers, we tell you early rather than late, and you choose: move to the next available date, continue at a revised small-group rate, or take a full refund of everything you have paid.
  • Solo journeys — Solo and tailor-made itineraries carry no group minimum at all. Your dates are confirmed the moment your deposit is received.
  • Visas — We prepare and submit your visa application on your behalf. Please note that the granting of a visa rests solely with the immigration authority of the country concerned, and is never guaranteed by us.
  • Medication — We arrange malaria prophylaxis, together with anti-nausea and anti-anxiety tablets, for your journey. Any prescription medicine should be confirmed with your own doctor before you travel — they remain the final authority on what is suitable for you.
  • Africa is not always hot — Despite the stereotype, Africa moves through distinct seasons and mornings at altitude can be genuinely cold. A hooded jumper is provided for cold conditions, and for every guest joining a hot-air balloon safari.
  • Dietary requirements — If you have strict dietary needs, please tell us at the time of booking. This includes vegans, guests in recovery, non-drinkers, and anyone following a prescription from their doctor.
  • Travel insurance — We facilitate travel insurance for our clients. If you are already covered, please say so during booking so we can take it into account in your final quote.
  • Tailoring your quote — Some of our inclusions — hot-air balloon safaris, for example — may not be what you want. Point these out and we will adjust your final offer accordingly.
  • The Maasai massage & spa recovery — Guests travelling the safari circuit by road rather than by air should expect long and tiring days. The rough terrain of the jungle gives the vehicle a constant jolting ride, known affectionately as the Maasai massage. A spa session is provided at the end of your safari to recover.
  • What to pack — Neutral colours are the rule on safari: khaki, olive, stone and brown. Avoid black and dark blue, which draw tsetse flies, and leave camouflage at home entirely — it is reserved for the military and illegal for civilians in several African countries. Bring layers for cold dawn game drives, long sleeves and trousers for dusk when the mosquitoes are out, closed walking shoes, a sun hat and sunglasses. Lodge dinners are smart-casual; nothing formal is needed.
  • Your safari duffel & kit — The duffel bag is used when flying into the parks, where luggage is limited to 15 kg per person. A branded {{brand}} safari hat and safari vest are also provided.
