Overview

Eighteen
Fifty-Two

A hundred and sixty-five rooms on the Bahnhofstrasse, in a village with no cars.

The Mont Cervin opened in 1852 with fourteen beds. The Seiler brothers leased it three years later and bought it in 1867, and by 1875 it had 180 — which is roughly the story of Zermatt itself compressed into twenty years. Alexander Seiler went on to build an empire of it, and the family name is still over the door. The 165 rooms split between the main building, where the Alpine-style rooms are brighter, with leather and wool, and the Petit Cervin, where the chalet rooms have rustic panelling and deep colours and read like small private chalets. The village outside has no combustion cars in it.

Zermatt is car-free. Vehicles stop at Täsch and the last five kilometres are by train. The hotel meets the station with an electric shuttle.
