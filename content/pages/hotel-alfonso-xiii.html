Overview

Nineteen
Twenty-Eight

A hundred and fifty-one rooms and suites around a Mudéjar courtyard.

King Alfonso XIII wanted somewhere to put the guests of the 1929 Ibero-American Exposition, and the brief was explicit: the grandest hotel in Europe. The competition was won by the Sevillian architect José Espiau y Muñoz, who built it in the neo-Mudéjar manner — horseshoe arches, exposed brick, wrought iron, ornamental towers and ceramic finials — around a great central courtyard with a fountain and orange trees. The King opened it himself on 28 April 1928. The 151 rooms keep the marble and wood floors and the tiled bathrooms they were built with, and some have Juliet balconies or private terraces.

The courtyard is the building. Everything is arranged around the central patio — mosaics, marble, a fountain and orange trees. It is where the hotel is at its best, at dusk.
