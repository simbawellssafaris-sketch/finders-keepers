OverviewThis is Botswana's other face — not water and floodplains, but salt and sand, an ancient sky and absolute silence. Over ten days, this circuit moves through the arid heart of southern Africa: the world's largest salt pans at Makgadikgadi, the sacred baobabs of Kubu Island rising from the pan floor, and five nights deep in the Central Kalahari Game Reserve — one of the largest and most remote protected areas on earth. Designed for travellers who have seen Africa's wildlife and now seek its deeper solitude and culture.

Itinerary

Gaborone

Day 1 | Arrival in Gaborone — First Night in Botswana

Touch down at Sir Seretse Khama International Airport in Gaborone. A {{brand}} representative greets you at arrivals and escorts you to your private guide transfer. The drive to the Protea Hotel by Marriott Gaborone Masa Square takes approximately twenty minutes through the city's wide avenues. Check into your suite — floor-to-ceiling windows, clean, modern lines, and views across the central square.

Your safari guide and local representative join you for a welcome briefing over dinner — maps, timelines, the Kalahari laid out before you. Your spa therapist notes your preferences for the aromatherapy deep-tissue massage scheduled at the end of the journey. A welcome dinner is served at the hotel's restaurant, and the night belongs to rest. Tomorrow, the pans begin.

Makgadikgadi Pans

Day 2 | Gaborone → Makgadikgadi Pans — Arrival at Leroo La Tau

After breakfast, your chauffeur transfers you to Gaborone Airport for a commercial flight to Maun — ninety minutes over the flat immensity of the Kalahari Basin. At Maun your guide and transfer vehicle are waiting. The drive to Leroo La Tau Lodge follows the Boteti River — one of this arid region's few permanent water sources — for approximately ninety minutes. Even before you reach the lodge, elephant, zebra and giraffe are likely to appear along the riverbank, the landscape already announcing itself.

Leroo La Tau — "place of the lion" in Setswana — sits on the bank of the Boteti River, overlooking the floodplain. Arrive for lunch on the open deck with views across the water. Your first game drive departs at 4:00 PM: elephant herds at the river's edge, hippos in the deeper pools, crocodiles on the sandbars. A sundowner on the riverbank as the sky explodes in orange and purple. Return to the lodge for a bonfire dinner under an enormous Botswana sky.

Leroo La Tau Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 3 | Makgadikgadi — Hot Air Balloon over the Salt Pans

Wake at 4:30 AM. Transfer to the balloon launch site on the edge of the Makgadikgadi Pans as the sky shifts from black to deep indigo. The balloon lifts above the pan floor at first light — and the world below becomes impossible: white to every horizon, so flat and so vast that the curvature of the earth is visible from the basket. The pans are the remains of a super-lake that once covered most of southern Africa — dried by climate change over millennia into this extraordinary white expanse. From above, the cracked salt polygons and occasional solitary baobab read like something from another planet.

After landing, a champagne bush breakfast is served on the pan floor itself — white linen, crystal glasses, a full breakfast prepared in the middle of the salt. The silence is a physical thing. After returning to the lodge, rest through the midday heat. At 4:00 PM, a game drive focuses on the Boteti River's lion prides — Leroo La Tau is named for them, and they rarely disappoint. A sundowner on the riverbank. Return to camp for a bonfire dinner.

Leroo La Tau Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 4 | Kubu Island — A Helicopter Excursion to the Ancient Baobabs

After breakfast, board a private helicopter for a flight to Kubu Island — one of Botswana's most sacred and visually extraordinary sites. The flight takes approximately thirty minutes, crossing the white expanse of the Makgadikgadi Pans at low level. From above, the scale of the salt flats becomes fully apparent: an inland sea of white stretching further than the eye can resolve.

Kubu Island is not a conventional island — it is a rocky granite outcrop rising from the pan floor, crowned with over a hundred ancient baobab trees, some more than a thousand years old. Their massive, twisted trunks create a landscape that feels as though it belongs to a different geological era. Your guide leads you among the baobabs, explaining the pan's history and the San people's sacred relationship with this place — their rock paintings still visible on some of the boulders. A private picnic lunch is served in the shade of the largest tree — champagne, gourmet food, and the white pan stretching to every horizon, the silence broken only by the wind. Return by helicopter to Leroo La Tau. A final sundowner on the riverbank. A bonfire dinner as the stars emerge over the Kalahari.

Leroo La Tau Lodge

Central Kalahari Game Reserve

Day 5 | Makgadikgadi → Central Kalahari — Into the Wilderness

After breakfast, board a light aircraft charter from Leroo La Tau to Tau Pan Lodge in the Central Kalahari Game Reserve — approximately one hour crossing the Kalahari Basin. The flight reveals the transition from the white pan floor to the golden grasslands and acacia woodlands of the Kalahari interior. Over 52,000 square kilometres with only a handful of lodges — this is among the most remote protected areas in Africa.

Tau Pan Lodge sits on the edge of an ancient dry lakebed, its raised decks offering uninterrupted views across the pan and the surrounding grasslands. Arrive for lunch, then settle into your suite with floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the wilderness. Your first Kalahari game drive departs at 4:00 PM — springbok pronking across the plains, gemsbok with their long straight horns catching the afternoon light, the Kalahari horizon perfectly flat in every direction. A sundowner on the pan floor. Return to the lodge for a bonfire dinner. The sky overhead is extraordinary — the Milky Way so dense and bright it requires a moment to comprehend.

Tau Pan Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 6 | Central Kalahari — Walking with the San

After breakfast, your San Bushman guide leads you into the acacia woodland on foot. He carries no rifle and needs none — he reads the bush the way a scholar reads a text, each footprint, birdcall, and bent grass stem a sentence in a language his people have spoken for 20,000 years. He shows you the tuber that holds emergency water in a dry riverbed, the berry that stings the tongue but cures a fever, the footprint of a brown hyena that passed two hours ago, the nest of a sociable weaver — a city of 400 birds in a single tree.

He demonstrates fire from two sticks — genuinely, without pretence. He shows you how to make a bow from a branch and a tendon. He explains the spiritual significance of the eland to the San cosmology — why the eland's fat is painted on ancient cave walls across southern Africa. The walk lasts two to three hours. Return to the lodge for lunch and a siesta. At 4:00 PM, a game drive across the Kalahari grasslands focuses on desert-adapted predators — the larger, paler lions of the Kalahari, and cheetahs hunting on the open plains. A sundowner on the pan. Bonfire dinner.

Tau Pan Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 7 | Central Kalahari — Full Day in the Field

Today is a full day given to the Kalahari's rhythms. Depart early with a packed lunch for an extended game drive covering the northern reaches of the reserve — the terrain shifting from grassland to acacia scrub to ancient dry riverbeds where the sand is golden and the silence absolute. The Kalahari's wildlife is nomadic and scattered, following the seasonal rains across distances that defy fixed routes. Your guide reads the land, not a map.

Mid-morning, a bush breakfast is set up in the shade of an acacia — coffee, eggs over an open fire, the sounds of the Kalahari all around. Lunch is a picnic in a dry riverbed. The afternoon continues the drive, heading back toward the lodge as the light softens to gold. A sundowner on the pan floor. A final bonfire dinner as the stars fill the sky from horizon to horizon.

Tau Pan Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 8 | Central Kalahari — San Cultural Immersion & Night Drive

This morning is set aside for the deeper cultural exchange that only extended time in one place makes possible. Your San hosts arrive after breakfast — not as performers, but as neighbours. They show you how ostrich eggshells become beads, how acacia wood becomes an arrow shaft, how sansevieria fibre is twisted into rope strong enough to hold a snare. The craftsmanship is unhurried and precise. They explain /Kaggen — the trickster mantis-god of San mythology, a figure of mischief, wisdom, and creation who appears when things fall apart and when they come together again.

Rest through the midday heat. At 4:00 PM, depart for a game drive that extends into darkness. As the sun sets, your guide switches to a spotlight and the Kalahari reveals its nocturnal layer: bat-eared foxes with their enormous ears tilted to the sand listening for termites, aardwolves grazing on the same termites above ground, porcupines rattling through the acacia, and if fortune allows, the elusive brown hyena — one of Africa's rarest large carnivores. A sundowner as the first stars appear. A late bonfire dinner under the full extent of the Kalahari sky.

Tau Pan Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 9 | Central Kalahari — A Final Day & Dinner Under the Stars

Your final full day in the Kalahari. After breakfast, a morning game drive with no agenda except to be present — to look, to listen, to absorb what nine days in this extraordinary landscape have taught. Rest through the midday heat. At 4:00 PM, your guide takes you to his favourite places in the reserve: the viewpoints where the pan stretches in every direction, the waterholes where gemsbok gather in the afternoon light, the ridges where the horizon is so flat and so far that Africa begins to feel like a planet seen from a great height.

Tonight, after the sundowner, a special bush dinner is laid out on the pan floor itself — a table set with white linen, lanterns placed in a circle in the sand, the lodge's chefs preparing a gourmet feast in the open air. You eat under the Kalahari sky as it wheels slowly overhead. It is an evening that stays with you. It is the reason people come back.

Gaborone

Day 10 | Central Kalahari → Gaborone — Spa, Farewell & Departure

After breakfast, board your light aircraft charter to Maun, then connect to a commercial flight back to Gaborone. The Kalahari recedes below you — the golden grassland, the ancient pan floor, the acacia woodland — and you carry it with you as the city appears ahead. Upon arrival, your private guide transfers you to the Protea Hotel by Marriott Gaborone Masa Square.

Your pre-arranged 90-minute aromatherapy deep-tissue massage is waiting at the Masa Square wellness centre — the therapist working with locally sourced ingredients: shea butter, marula oil, and extracts from the desert melon that the San have used as a moisturiser for generations. It is the perfect transition from the bush. A farewell dinner at the hotel's rooftop restaurant as the city lights spread below, your guide joining you for a final evening. Your airport transfer is timed precisely to your international departure — whether your flight leaves that evening or the following morning, you will be at the airport when you need to be, unhurried and well-rested.

Journey Details

Minimum Age

Varies by camp, activity and national park

  • Camps and lodges — Most safari camps accept children from six years, with shared game-drive vehicles usually from eight to twelve. Many properties will take younger children on a privately booked vehicle.
  • Walking safaris — Guided walking safaris carry a firm minimum of fifteen or sixteen years across most reserves. This is set by the licensing authority, not the camp, and is never waived.
  • Gorilla and chimpanzee trekking — Uganda and Rwanda both enforce a strict minimum of fifteen years. It is a national park regulation and there are no exceptions.
  • Hot-air balloon safaris — Operators generally require children to be at least seven, and tall enough to brace unaided in the basket on landing.
  • Family and exclusive-use camps — A number of our camps run dedicated family units, child-minding and tailored activity programmes, and will relax their usual age limit for a sole-use booking.
  • Talk to us first — Age limits vary from property to property and from activity to activity. Speak to our experts before you book, and we will pre-arrange the details with each lodge around the ages of the children travelling with you.

Safari Duffel

Duffel, safari hat, vest and hooded jumper

  • Your duffel — A soft-sided {{brand}} duffel, sized for the 15 kg limit on the light aircraft that move you between camps. Rigid suitcases cannot be carried on those flights at all.
  • What is inside it — A branded {{brand}} safari hat, a safari vest, and a hooded jumper for cold dawn starts and balloon mornings.
  • Yours to keep — The bag and everything in it is a gift, not a loan. Nothing is returned at the end of your journey.
  • Packing around it — Soft luggage, neutral colours, and layers. Laundry is included at nearly every camp, which is why 15 kg goes further than it sounds.

Note

Water levels shape the Okavango Delta experience

The Delta's flood waters typically peak between June and August, affecting which activities (mokoro, boating, or 4x4 game drives) are available at each camp. Malaria prophylaxis is recommended.

  • Early starts — Safaris begin very early in the morning. Your guide will confirm what time you will be collected from your camp or lodge.
  • Tailoring this journey — If you would like this package with more or fewer days, arranged for a group, a family or a solo traveller, or with add-ons, reach out and we will tailor it for you.
  • Route changes — Your guide or driver may alter the route in certain circumstances, but every destination set out in the itinerary will be covered.

Documents

Visa-free for most nationalities

  • Passport — Valid for at least six months beyond your arrival date, with at least two blank pages for stamps.
  • Visa — Visa-free for up to 90 days for UK, EU, US, Canadian, Australian and most Commonwealth and SADC passport holders. Other nationalities apply through the Botswana eVisa portal before travel.
  • Travelling with children — An unabridged birth certificate showing both parents is required for every traveller under 18. Where a child travels with one parent only, or with neither, a sworn parental consent affidavit is also needed.
  • Light-aircraft weight declaration — Camp-to-camp flights in the Delta require the actual body weight of every passenger in advance. It is a safety calculation for small aircraft, not a formality, and we must submit it with your booking.
  • We file it for you — Whether you are flying in from overseas or travelling from elsewhere on the continent, we complete and submit your visa application on your behalf. You hand us your documents once. We do the filing, chase the approval, and carry the confirmations in your travel pack so nothing rests on you remembering a reference number at a counter.
  • Most of Africa is open to you — The great majority of our African destinations are now either visa-free or issue a visa on arrival, so an embassy queue is rarely in your future at all. Where a visa on arrival does apply, we prepare and lodge the paperwork ahead of you — you reach the desk with a finished file rather than a blank form and a biro.
  • Before you travel — Entry rules change frequently and without much notice. Your specialist confirms the current requirements for every passport in your party at the time of booking, and checks them again before you fly.

Guaranteed Departures

Private, tailor-made departures on your dates, or join a small group

  • Once confirmed, you travel — When your departure date is confirmed, it is guaranteed. We do not cancel, merge or reschedule a confirmed departure for commercial reasons, however the final numbers fall.
  • What confirms a departure — A departure becomes confirmed once the agreed number of travellers has been reached and paid for in line with the agreed payment schedule. Places held without payment do not count towards it.
  • If the minimum is not reached — Where a group departure has not reached its agreed numbers, we tell you early rather than late, and you choose: move to the next available date, continue at a revised small-group rate, or take a full refund of everything you have paid.
  • Solo journeys — Solo and tailor-made itineraries carry no group minimum at all. Your dates are confirmed the moment your deposit is received.
  • Visas — We prepare and submit your visa application on your behalf. Please note that the granting of a visa rests solely with the immigration authority of the country concerned, and is never guaranteed by us.
  • Medication — We arrange malaria prophylaxis, together with anti-nausea and anti-anxiety tablets, for your journey. Any prescription medicine should be confirmed with your own doctor before you travel — they remain the final authority on what is suitable for you.
  • Africa is not always hot — Despite the stereotype, Africa moves through distinct seasons and mornings at altitude can be genuinely cold. A hooded jumper is provided for cold conditions, and for every guest joining a hot-air balloon safari.
  • Dietary requirements — If you have strict dietary needs, please tell us at the time of booking. This includes vegans, guests in recovery, non-drinkers, and anyone following a prescription from their doctor.
  • Travel insurance — We facilitate travel insurance for our clients. If you are already covered, please say so during booking so we can take it into account in your final quote.
  • Tailoring your quote — Some of our inclusions — hot-air balloon safaris, for example — may not be what you want. Point these out and we will adjust your final offer accordingly.
  • The Maasai massage & spa recovery — Guests travelling the safari circuit by road rather than by air should expect long and tiring days. The rough terrain of the jungle gives the vehicle a constant jolting ride, known affectionately as the Maasai massage. A spa session is provided at the end of your safari to recover.
  • What to pack — Neutral colours are the rule on safari: khaki, olive, stone and brown. Avoid black and dark blue, which draw tsetse flies, and leave camouflage at home entirely — it is reserved for the military and illegal for civilians in several African countries. Bring layers for cold dawn game drives, long sleeves and trousers for dusk when the mosquitoes are out, closed walking shoes, a sun hat and sunglasses. Lodge dinners are smart-casual; nothing formal is needed.
  • Your safari duffel & kit — The duffel bag is used when flying into the parks, where luggage is limited to 15 kg per person. A branded {{brand}} safari hat and safari vest are also provided.
