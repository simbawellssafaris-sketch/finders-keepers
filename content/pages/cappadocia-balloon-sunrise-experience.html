Cappadocia, Türkiye

Rise over the fairy chimneys at sunrise by balloon

A hundred and more balloons lifting together at first light over a valley of eroded volcanic spires, cut into with churches and houses. It is the most flown balloon site on earth, and the reason is the terrain.

The landscape is ash and tuff from eruptions of Erciyes and Hasan Dağı, weathered into cones, ridges and the capped columns known as fairy chimneys. Because the rock is soft, people carved into it — dwellings, monasteries, painted rock churches, and whole underground cities running many levels down.

Balloons launch before sunrise because the air is at its most stable and the light is at its best. Pilots work the wind at different altitudes to steer, dropping into the valleys to drift between the formations and then climbing for the wider view over Göreme and the Rose Valley.

Flights end with the traditional landing toast. The whole thing runs about an hour in the air, and rather longer either side.

Weather cancels flights, routinely

The civil aviation authority grounds all balloons in wind, fog or rain and it happens often — so we build at least two, ideally three, mornings into Cappadocia rather than one. Pick-up is around four in the morning and it is cold before dawn even in summer, whatever the afternoon does. Flat closed shoes for the landing.
