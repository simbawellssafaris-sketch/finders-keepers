Overview

Eleven
Eighty-Four

A hundred and forty-four rooms and thirty-five suites inside a UNESCO-listed monument.

The Hôtel-Dieu was built in 1184, became a working hospital in the 15th century, and did not stop being one until 2010 — Rabelais was its chief physician in 1532. The riverside wing was designed by Jacques-Germain Soufflot, the architect of the Paris Panthéon, and runs for 375 metres along the Rhône beneath the dome that finished the building in the 18th century. The conversion kept the bones: the 144 rooms and 35 suites have the exceptional ceiling heights the wards were built with, and most of them look out over the river.

The ceilings are the tell. The rooms were hospital wards, and the extraordinary ceiling heights are original. Nothing built as a hotel in Lyon has them.
