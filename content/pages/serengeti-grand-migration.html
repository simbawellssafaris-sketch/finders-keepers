OverviewThis twelve-day journey follows one of the greatest wildlife spectacles on earth — the Great Migration of more than 1.5 million wildebeest, zebra and gazelle across the plains of Tanzania. From the elephant and baobab landscapes of Tarangire to the volcanic grandeur of the Ngorongoro Crater, the endless golden plains of the central Serengeti and the dramatic river crossings of the north, this circuit is the definitive East African safari, combining legendary wildlife with exceptional camps and intimate small-group access.

Itinerary

Arusha, Tanzania

Day 1 | Arrive Arusha — Gateway to the Serengeti

Touch down at Kilimanjaro International Airport. Your {{brand}} representative meets you in the arrivals hall — no searching, no queuing. You are escorted through formalities and into a waiting transfer vehicle for the short journey to your Arusha lodge, set on the slopes of Mount Meru. If your flight arrives before midday, enjoy a complimentary afternoon excursion to a Maasai market or a coffee plantation tour showcasing Tanzania's celebrated Arabica coffee from harvest to cup. As evening approaches, the group gathers for a welcome briefing and dinner. Your guide spreads maps on the table, outlines the route and shares what to expect from the migration. Mount Kilimanjaro gleams in the late light. Tomorrow, the wilderness begins.

Tarangire National Park

Day 2 | Arusha → Tarangire — Elephants & Ancient Baobabs

After breakfast, depart Arusha by road for Tarangire National Park (approximately 2.5 hours). The drive carries you across the wide Masai Steppe and through the towns of Makuyuni and Kwa Kuchinja. Enter Tarangire through its northern gate. Tarangire is perhaps the most underestimated park in Tanzania — and that is its greatest gift. The Tarangire River draws the largest concentration of wildlife outside the Serengeti during the dry season, with elephant populations rivalling anything on earth. The ancient baobab trees — some more than a thousand years old — dominate the skyline, their vast girth evidence of centuries of survival. An afternoon game drive explores the river circuit: elephants in their hundreds wading into the shallows, lion prides resting in the shade, oryx, eland, lesser kudu and fringe-eared oryx moving through the woodland. Arrive at your camp in time for a sundowner and dinner under the stars.

Day 3 | Tarangire — Full Day Safari & Walking Safari

A dawn game drive through Tarangire's southern sector, into the Silale Swamp — a mosaic of swamp, riverine forest and open grassland that harbours unusual species rarely seen elsewhere: greater kudu, fringe-eared oryx and the elusive gerenuk. Mid-morning, your vehicle pulls into a shaded clearing for a walking safari with an armed ranger. On foot, the scale of the bush becomes tangible. Your guide reads tracks in the dust — a pride of lions passed here at dawn, an elephant bull rested under this fig tree, a leopard dragged its kill across this path three days ago. Return to camp for lunch and a siesta. A late afternoon game drive explores the river flats as elephants gather to drink in the golden light. Sundowner on the river escarpment as the sun sinks behind the baobabs. Dinner by firelight.

Ngorongoro Conservation Area

Day 4 | Tarangire → Ngorongoro — The World's Greatest Caldera

After breakfast, check out and depart for the Ngorongoro Conservation Area (approximately 4 hours via Karatu). The road climbs through the fertile farmlands of the Crater Highlands — coffee, tea and banana plantations clinging to the steep slopes. Arrive at the crater rim in the late morning. The view from the rim is one of the most breathtaking in Africa: a perfectly circular caldera 19 kilometres across, its floor a mosaic of grassland, lake and acacia woodland, enclosing an entire ecosystem within its walls. The crater shelters some 25,000 large animals — lions, leopards, elephants, buffalo, hippos, hyenas, black rhinos and an extraordinary concentration of wildebeest and zebra. Check into your rim lodge — perched on the crater's edge, with uninterrupted views across the caldera. Lunch at the lodge. A late afternoon game drive on the crater rim, watching the light fade over one of Africa's most extraordinary landscapes. Dinner at the lodge.

Day 5 | Ngorongoro — Full Day Crater Descent & Walking Safari

An early morning descent into the crater. The road drops steeply through the forest zone — a micro-climate of dense vegetation where elephant and buffalo move between the trees. Reach the crater floor as the morning mist lifts. The crater is a predator's paradise. Within the first hour: a pride of lions at a kill, hyenas circling at a respectful distance. Your guide drives toward Lake Magadi — a shallow soda lake tinged pink by hundreds of flamingos. Black rhinos — among the rarest animals in Africa — move across the crater floor in the middle distance. In the afternoon, a walking safari with an armed Maasai ranger on the outer slopes of the crater, descending into the acacia woodland where buffalo and elephant roam freely. Return to the rim at sunset. A final bonfire dinner on the crater rim.

Central Serengeti

Day 6 | Ngorongoro → Central Serengeti — The Endless Plains

After breakfast, depart the crater rim for the Serengeti (approximately 3 hours including game drive en route). The road descends from the highlands into the vast open grasslands of the Serengeti. Cross the Naabi Hill Gate and the landscape opens up in every direction — the Serengeti is, in Maasai, "the place where the land runs on forever." And it does. The central Serengeti's Seronera Valley is the year-round heartland of the ecosystem — resident lion prides, leopards draped across riverine fig trees, cheetahs on termite mounds scanning the plain. En route to camp, a game drive through the valley: a large lion coalition near the Seronera River, a leopard with a kill in the fig trees above, a cheetah making a high-speed pursuit across the open plains. Arrive at your camp in time for sundowners. Dinner by torchlight on the open plains. The sound of hyenas in the darkness.

Day 7 | Central Serengeti — Hot Air Balloon Safari & Full Day on the Plains

The alarm comes at 4:30 AM. A short drive to the launch site on the open plains. The balloon inflates in the darkness — the burners lighting the fabric orange in the pre-dawn. You climb in. The ground falls away. Below you, the Serengeti stretches to every horizon — an ocean of grass under a sky full of fading stars. As the sun rises over the escarpment to the east, the plains ignite in gold and amber. Wildebeest columns move across the plain in their thousands. Lions pad back from a night hunt. A cheetah moves through the long grass. The balloon drifts for over an hour. You descend to a champagne breakfast on the open plains — white linen, silver, a chef working at a bush kitchen as the day warms. A full morning game drive follows. An afternoon at leisure at the camp before a final Seronera Valley drive as the light turns amber. Sundowner. Night game drive. Bonfire dinner.

Day 8 | Central Serengeti — Predator Safari & Big Cat Tracking

A full day dedicated to the Serengeti's legendary predator population. The Seronera Valley holds one of the highest densities of big cats on earth. Your guide connects via radio with the Serengeti Lion Project researchers, who share the morning's big cat locations. Dawn game drive: a coalition of four male lions at a buffalo kill, hyenas and vultures queuing at a respectful distance. Mid-morning: a mother cheetah with three cubs on a termite mound, scanning the plain. After a bush lunch in the shade of an acacia, an afternoon in search of the valley's resident leopards — some of the most habituated and photographed leopards anywhere in Africa. Sundowner at a kopje — a granite outcrop beloved by lions and hyrax alike, offering a 360-degree view across the endless plain. Night game drive. Dinner by starlight.

Northern Serengeti

Day 9 | Central → Northern Serengeti — The Migration Awaits

After breakfast, a game drive north through the Serengeti. The landscape shifts as you move north: the short grass plains of the centre give way to longer, golden savannah, rolling hills and the acacia woodlands of the Lobo Valley. And then you see them. A column of wildebeest — tens of thousands of them — moving north in single file, their distinctive grunting filling the air. The Great Migration. More than 1.5 million animals following their ancient instinct, moving toward the Mara River and the pastures beyond. Arrive at your northern Serengeti camp in time for lunch — a mobile tented camp positioned to provide the best possible access to the crossing points. An afternoon game drive to the Mara River: the river is high, fast-moving. Wildebeest cluster on the far bank, milling nervously. The question is when, not if. Return to camp as the sun sets over the Mara. Dinner under the stars, the rumble of the migration audible in the distance.

Day 10 | Northern Serengeti — The River Crossing

The day begins before dawn. You are at the Mara River before first light, positioned at a crossing point your guide has identified the previous evening. The sun rises. The wildebeest mass on the far bank — thousands of them, pressing forward. Then a leader plunges in. And then the world erupts. A torrent of wildebeest surges into the water. The river churns white. Crocodiles explode from the shallows. The noise is overwhelming — hooves, grunting, the sound of ten thousand animals driven by instinct toward the far bank. Some make it. Some do not. The crossing lasts twenty minutes. You watch in silence. A full day follows along the Mara River circuit, watching for repeat crossings and tracking the predators — lions, leopards and cheetahs — that shadow the migration. Return to camp. Sundowner on a river bluff, the migration stretching to the horizon. Bonfire dinner. This is the moment people come to Africa for.

Day 11 | Northern Serengeti — A Final Day with the Migration

A final full day in the northern Serengeti. Your guide asks what you most wish to see or revisit: the crossing points, the predator corridors or the vast herds moving through the Lamai Wedge to the north. You choose the river one last time. The morning brings another crossing — perhaps smaller than yesterday's, but no less extraordinary. Lions have followed the herds and made a kill on the near bank: a pride of eight lionesses and two large males, gorging while vultures wheel above and jackals wait at the edges. A bush lunch in the shade. Afternoon: the migration columns on the move north, the sound of a hundred thousand hooves on dry earth. Your guide sets up a sundowner on a kopje as the sun descends over the Mara. A farewell bonfire dinner back at camp. Tomorrow, you return south — but you carry the crossing with you for life.

Arusha / Departure

Day 12 | Northern Serengeti → Arusha — Farewell to the Plains

An early morning game drive through the northern Serengeti — the light is extraordinary at this hour and the plains are active. Breakfast at camp. Then a bush flight from the Kogatende or Lamai airstrip to Arusha's Kilimanjaro International Airport (approximately 1 hour 30 minutes). From above, the Serengeti stretches away below you — the wildebeest columns visible from the air, tiny dark rivers moving across an ocean of gold. You land at Kilimanjaro and are transferred to the airport for your international departure. Your guide hands you a small gift — a memento of twelve extraordinary days. You promise to return. Journey Details

Minimum Age

Varies by camp, activity and national park

  • Camps and lodges — Most safari camps accept children from six years, with shared game-drive vehicles usually from eight to twelve. Many properties will take younger children on a privately booked vehicle.
  • Walking safaris — Guided walking safaris carry a firm minimum of fifteen or sixteen years across most reserves. This is set by the licensing authority, not the camp, and is never waived.
  • Gorilla and chimpanzee trekking — Uganda and Rwanda both enforce a strict minimum of fifteen years. It is a national park regulation and there are no exceptions.
  • Hot-air balloon safaris — Operators generally require children to be at least seven, and tall enough to brace unaided in the basket on landing.
  • Family and exclusive-use camps — A number of our camps run dedicated family units, child-minding and tailored activity programmes, and will relax their usual age limit for a sole-use booking.
  • Talk to us first — Age limits vary from property to property and from activity to activity. Speak to our experts before you book, and we will pre-arrange the details with each lodge around the ages of the children travelling with you.

Safari Duffel

Duffel, safari hat, vest and hooded jumper

  • Your duffel — A soft-sided {{brand}} duffel, sized for the 15 kg limit on the light aircraft that move you between camps. Rigid suitcases cannot be carried on those flights at all.
  • What is inside it — A branded {{brand}} safari hat, a safari vest, and a hooded jumper for cold dawn starts and balloon mornings.
  • Yours to keep — The bag and everything in it is a gift, not a loan. Nothing is returned at the end of your journey.
  • Packing around it — Soft luggage, neutral colours, and layers. Laundry is included at nearly every camp, which is why 15 kg goes further than it sounds.

Note

Migration timing varies with seasonal rains

The wildebeest migration moves across the Serengeti ecosystem on a roughly annual cycle driven by rainfall, so exact river-crossing locations shift year to year. Malaria prophylaxis is recommended for all safari areas.

  • Early starts — Safaris begin very early in the morning. Your guide will confirm what time you will be collected from your camp or lodge.
  • Tailoring this journey — If you would like this package with more or fewer days, arranged for a group, a family or a solo traveller, or with add-ons, reach out and we will tailor it for you.
  • Route changes — Your guide or driver may alter the route in certain circumstances, but every destination set out in the itinerary will be covered.

Documents

Visa on arrival or e-Visa, from $50

  • Passport — Valid for at least six months beyond your arrival date, with at least two blank pages for stamps.
  • Visa — Apply for the eVisa online (one to three working days) or take a visa on arrival at the main airports and land borders. Single entry is US$50. US passport holders are issued the US$100 multiple-entry visa instead.
  • One visa, both halves — A single Tanzanian visa covers the mainland safari circuit and the Zanzibar archipelago. You do not need a second visa to fly to the islands.
  • Yellow fever — A vaccination certificate is required if you are arriving from, or have transited, a country with yellow fever risk.
  • We file it for you — Whether you are flying in from overseas or travelling from elsewhere on the continent, we complete and submit your visa application on your behalf. You hand us your documents once. We do the filing, chase the approval, and carry the confirmations in your travel pack so nothing rests on you remembering a reference number at a counter.
  • Most of Africa is open to you — The great majority of our African destinations are now either visa-free or issue a visa on arrival, so an embassy queue is rarely in your future at all. Where a visa on arrival does apply, we prepare and lodge the paperwork ahead of you — you reach the desk with a finished file rather than a blank form and a biro.
  • Before you travel — Entry rules change frequently and without much notice. Your specialist confirms the current requirements for every passport in your party at the time of booking, and checks them again before you fly.

Guaranteed Departures

Guaranteed to operate with a minimum of 4 guests

  • Once confirmed, you travel — When your departure date is confirmed, it is guaranteed. We do not cancel, merge or reschedule a confirmed departure for commercial reasons, however the final numbers fall.
  • What confirms a departure — A departure becomes confirmed once the agreed number of travellers has been reached and paid for in line with the agreed payment schedule. Places held without payment do not count towards it.
  • If the minimum is not reached — Where a group departure has not reached its agreed numbers, we tell you early rather than late, and you choose: move to the next available date, continue at a revised small-group rate, or take a full refund of everything you have paid.

  • Africa is not always hot — Despite the stereotype, Africa moves through distinct seasons and mornings at altitude can be genuinely cold. A hooded jumper is provided for cold conditions, and for every guest joining a hot-air balloon safari.
  • Dietary requirements — If you have strict dietary needs, please tell us at the time of booking. This includes vegans, guests in recovery, non-drinkers, and anyone following a prescription from their doctor.

  • The Maasai massage & spa recovery — Guests travelling the safari circuit by road rather than by air should expect long and tiring days.   • What to pack — Neutral colours are the rule on safari: khaki, olive, stone and brown. Avoid black and dark blue, which draw tsetse flies, and leave camouflage at home entirely — it is reserved for the military and illegal for civilians in several African countries. Bring layers for cold dawn game drives, long sleeves and trousers for dusk when the mosquitoes are out, closed walking shoes, a sun hat and sunglasses. Lodge dinners are smart-casual; nothing formal is needed.
