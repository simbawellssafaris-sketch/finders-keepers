Overview

The Only Lodge
on the Skeleton Coast

Shipwreck Lodge is the sole property built inside Skeleton Coast National Park — officially the least visited of all of Namibia’s parks, and among the most remote coastlines on the continent.

Designed by Windhoek-based Nina Maritz Architects, Shipwreck Lodge takes its form from the more than a thousand ships that have run aground along this stretch of Atlantic coastline over the centuries. Each of its ten timber-and-glass cabins is built to evoke a broken section of hull — abstracted, weathered, and angled into the dune ridge near the dry mouth of the Hoarusib River. In 2025 the lodge received Two Keys in the inaugural MICHELIN Guide Global Keys Selection, recognising design, service and location of the very highest order. Every structure is solar-powered, prefabricated off-site, and built to be fully removable at the end of its 25-year concession — a lodge designed to leave no permanent mark on one of Earth's most fragile landscapes.

Experiences

Where the Desert

Activities here trade the expected safari drive for shipwrecks, seal colonies, and desert-adapted wildlife found nowhere else on Earth.

History

Suiderkus & Karimona Wrecks

Visit the remains of two notable shipwrecks that gave the Skeleton Coast its name, testament to the power of this Atlantic stretch.

Wildlife

Möwe Bay Seal Colony

A full-day 4x4 excursion reaches the seal colony at Möwe Bay, passing the abandoned Westies diamond mine en route.

Tracking

Desert Elephant & Lion

Game drives through the park track the coast's rare desert-adapted elephant, lion and brown hyena.

Geology

The Clay Castles

Explore the Hoarusib River’s remarkable Clay Castles — cliff-like formations carved by water over millennia.

Guided walks across the dunes reveal beetles, succulents and the tracks of the coast’s nocturnal residents.

Morning quad biking and sandboarding sessions make the most of the towering dune fields around camp.

Location

The Skeleton
Coast

Shipwreck Lodge sits in the Skeleton Coast Central Concession Area, near the dry mouth of the Hoarusib River, in Namibia’s remote northwest.

  • The Skeleton Coast National Park covers 16,845km² and is officially the least-visited of Namibia’s protected areas.
  • Self-drivers are not permitted within the park — guests are collected by 4x4 at Mowe Bay for the final leg of the journey.
  • The lodge is a joint venture between Natural Selection, local Namibian operators, and the communities bordering the park.
  • Proceeds from every stay support ongoing research into the coast’s desert-adapted brown hyena and lion populations.
Fly

Light aircraft from Windhoek land at Möwe Bay airstrip, the most convenient way to reach this remote coastline.

Drive

Self-drive as far as Möwe Bay is possible (roughly 8 hours from Swakopmund), where lodge staff take over for the final approach.

Arrive

A 4x4 transfer from Möwe Bay covers the last stretch into the dunes — self-driving inside the park is not permitted.
