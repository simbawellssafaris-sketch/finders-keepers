Overview

The Stolen
River

When David Livingstone first saw the Savute Channel in 1851, it was flowing. Thirty years later it had vanished — the first chapter in a mystery that has puzzled researchers ever since.

The channel has flowed and dried independent of rainfall or flood patterns elsewhere for generations, giving it a reputation as the river that runs in both directions. It began flowing again in 2009, spilling into the Savute Marsh by 2010 for the first time in three decades. Savute Safari Lodge sits directly on its banks in the western reaches of Chobe National Park, with just eleven chalets and one family chalet capping occupancy at 24 guests — an intimate footprint in a region famous for its brutal contests between lion and hyena over elephant kills.

Second-largest zebra migration in Africa. Savute lies on the path of Africa's second-largest zebra migration, typically passing through between November and December, and again from February to April, depending on the rains.

Location

The Western
Reaches of Chobe

Savute sits in the western section of Chobe National Park, Botswana's first national park and its third largest, where the Savute Channel forms the region's defining feature.

  • The Savute Channel runs from the Linyanti River to the Savute Marsh, flowing and drying independent of rainfall elsewhere.
  • Chobe National Park, established in 1967, is thought to hold the largest elephant herds of any park in the world.
  • Night drives, walking safaris and off-road driving are not permitted in this part of the park, preserving its wilderness character.
  • Two pumped waterholes in front of the lodge draw elephants in large numbers, especially in the morning and evening.
Fly

Light aircraft charters connect to an airstrip near Savute, followed by a short game-drive transfer.

Drive

A road transfer of roughly three-and-a-half hours from Kasane is also possible.

Combine

Savute pairs naturally with Camp Moremi or Chobe Game Lodge for a wider Chobe and Moremi circuit.

Migration Season

Visit between November and December, or February and April, to coincide with Africa's second-largest zebra migration passing directly by the lodge.
