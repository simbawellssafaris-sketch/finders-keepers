Overview

A Temple,
Not a Hotel

With roots stretching back to the Heian era and its current form established by a disciple of Koyasan's founding tradition, Soji-in sits just west of Kongobu-ji, the head temple of Shingon Buddhism, and has welcomed overnight guests since 1994.

Rooms here are Japanese in style, with tatami floors and futon bedding, and — unusually for Koyasan's temple lodgings — most include a private Western-style toilet, with some offering a full private bathroom and air conditioning, comforts not every shukubo on the mountain provides. Shojin ryori, the elaborate vegetarian cuisine developed by Buddhist monks over centuries, is served directly in your room by a member of the temple, using no meat, fish, onion or garlic yet built from an extraordinary range of textures and flavours. Guests are welcome to join the monks each morning from around 6am for chanted prayers in the temple's main hall, an experience repeat visitors often describe as the reason they returned to Koyasan at all.

Please respect this is a place of worship first. Soji-in remains a functioning Buddhist temple. Meals must typically be requested by 1pm on the day of arrival, and guests are asked to arrive by early evening in keeping with the temple's own schedule.

Experiences

Sutra Copying,

A stay at Soji-in is built around the temple's own rhythm — meals, prayer and quiet reflection rather than a checklist of sights.

Ritual

Morning Prayer Service

Guests are welcome to join the temple's monks from around 6am for chanted sutras in the main hall, no reservation required.

Practice

Sutra Copying (Shakyo)

A meditative session of hand-copying Buddhist scripture, available at some Koyasan temples including Soji-in, for an additional fee.

Nearby

Kongobu-ji Temple

The head temple of Shingon Buddhism sits just steps from Soji-in, its rock garden among the largest in Japan.

Heritage

Danjo Garan

A complex of pagodas and halls forming the spiritual centre of Koyasan, a short walk from the temple.

Pilgrimage

Okuno-in Cemetery

Japan's largest cemetery, home to over 200,000 memorials along a moss-covered forest path, reachable on foot or by bus.

Reflection

Location

The Centre
of Koyasan

Soji-in sits just west of Kongobu-ji, the head temple of Shingon Buddhism, in front of the Danjo Garan complex at the heart of Koyasan's UNESCO World Heritage temple town.

  • Located to the west of Sohonzan Kongobu-ji, directly in front of the Danjo Garan complex.
  • All destinations in Koyasan village are within easy walking distance, connected also by local bus.
  • Okuno-in Cemetery, Japan's largest, is roughly a 30-minute walk away.
  • Kansai International Airport is the nearest major airport, roughly 25 miles from Koyasan.
Fly

Kansai International Airport is roughly 25 miles from Koyasan, connected via train and cable car.

Rail

The Nankai Koya Line and Koyasan Cable Car connect Osaka's Namba Station to Koyasan in around 90 minutes to two hours.

Arrive

A local bus network connects Koyasan's cable car station to the village and Soji-in itself.
