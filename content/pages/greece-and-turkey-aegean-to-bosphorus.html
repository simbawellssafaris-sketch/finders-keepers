Itinerary

Athens

Day 1 | Arrival in Athens

Land at Athens International and transfer to the Grande Bretagne, overlooking Syntagma Square and the Parliament. The Acropolis is visible from most of the city, which is the point — you are never allowed to forget what happened here.

The evening walks Plaka, the oldest quarter, a warren of neoclassical streets beneath the rock; then Monastiraki and its flea market; then up the steps into Anafiotika, a tiny Cycladic village built into the hillside under the Acropolis by island builders who were homesick.

Dinner is Greek, with the floodlit Acropolis in view.

Hotel Grande Bretagne
Dinner

Day 2 | The Acropolis & Ancient Athens

Up the sacred rock with a private guide to the Parthenon, the temple to Athena that defines the golden age of Pericles and, arguably, Western architecture. From the top the view runs across the whole city to the Saronic Gulf.

The Acropolis Museum follows, built above a live archaeological excavation with glass floors over the ruins, holding the original Caryatids and the Parthenon frieze.

The afternoon is the Ancient Agora — the marketplace and civic centre where Socrates walked and democracy was argued into being — then the Roman Agora, the all-marble Panathenaic Stadium that hosted the first modern Olympics in 1896, and the Temple of Olympian Zeus, of whose 104 columns fifteen still stand. Dinner is meze.

Hotel Grande Bretagne
Breakfast, Dinner
Santorini

Day 3 | Athens → Santorini

Transfer to Piraeus, the largest passenger port in Greece, and take the high-speed ferry to Santorini — four to five hours with the Aegean islands passing all the way.

Your transfer climbs to Oia and Canaves Oia, built directly into the volcano wall of the caldera. The afternoon explores Oia itself: whitewashed Cycladic houses and blue domes stacked down the cliff, narrow streets, boutiques and the caldera viewpoints.

The sunset is the reason Oia is famous. As the sun drops into the Aegean the caldera turns gold and red and the whole cliff lights up. Dinner is timed for it.

Canaves Oia Hotel
Breakfast, Dinner

Day 4 | Santorini by Sea

A private boat around the caldera — the flooded crater of one of the largest volcanic eruptions in recorded history.

Nea Kameni first, in the centre: a walk across black lava fields to the smoking crater, where steam still rises. Then Palea Kameni, and the hot springs — warm, iron- and sulphur-rich water that has drawn bathers for centuries and will stain a pale swimsuit permanently.

Thirassia follows, a small island that was part of Santorini before the eruption, for a Mediterranean lunch in a traditional village looking back across the caldera. Swimming in the afternoon, often in a cove reachable only by boat.

Canaves Oia Hotel
Breakfast, Lunch

Day 5 | Villages & Volcanic Wine

Santorini away from the caldera edge. Pyrgos was the island’s capital, and the climb to its Venetian Kasteli gives the widest view on the island. Megalochori is quieter still — Cycladic architecture, bell towers and a Venetian tower, and almost no one.

Then the vineyards. Santorini’s volcanic soil and relentless wind produce some of the most distinctive wine in Greece, and the vines are trained into low basket shapes to shelter the grapes. A private tasting focuses on Assyrtiko, the island’s white grape, with its mineral and citrus edge.

Lunch at a local taverna, and a final free evening in Oia.

Canaves Oia Hotel
Breakfast, Lunch
Chios

Day 6 | Santorini → Chios

This is where the journey stops resembling anyone else’s. Depending on the season, you travel from Santorini to Chios by fast ferry or by air, usually connecting through another island — and the route itself is a rare look at an archipelago most visitors never cross.

Chios is the fifth-largest Greek island, famous for mastic, and it sits just off the Turkish coast. Check in at the Chios Chandris, at the end of a natural bay and walking distance from the town.

The afternoon explores Chios Town, its medieval castle built on an ancient citadel, and the old port where the fishing boats and the Turkey ferries share the water. Dinner is Greek, with the island’s mastic-flavoured desserts and local wine.

Chios Chandris Hotel
Breakfast, Dinner
Istanbul

Day 7 | Chios → Çešme → Istanbul

To Chios Port for the international ferry to Turkey. The crossing is 18 kilometres and about twenty minutes, with up to five sailings a day — short, but it is the moment the journey changes. Greece recedes behind you and the Turkish coast at Çešme comes up ahead.

Through immigration into Turkey. From Çešme you continue to Istanbul either by private car to Izmir Airport, about an hour, and a domestic flight of roughly an hour and fifteen; or, for those who prefer the overland version, by private vehicle with stops along the way.

Check in at the Four Seasons at Sultanahmet, a neoclassical building of 1918–19 that was Istanbul’s first modern prison and is now one of its most distinctive addresses, arranged around an enclosed garden courtyard. Dinner at AVLU, the hotel’s Michelin-recommended Anatolian restaurant.

Four Seasons Hotel Istanbul at Sultanahmet
Breakfast, Dinner

Day 8 | Istanbul, the Two-Continent City

Everything today is within walking distance. Hagia Sophia first: built in the sixth century as a cathedral, converted to a mosque in 1453, and a symbol of the city’s layered history for fifteen hundred years. Then the Blue Mosque across the square, with its six minarets and twenty thousand handmade tiles — go early, before prayer times.

The Hippodrome was the chariot-racing heart of Constantinople; the Egyptian Obelisk, the Serpentine Column and the Fountain of Wilhelm II still stand on it. Below ground, the Basilica Cistern holds 336 marble columns in the dark, two of them resting on Medusa heads.

Topkapı Palace closes the morning — four hundred years of Ottoman sultans, the imperial treasury, and pavilions looking out over the Bosphorus. The afternoon is the Grand Bazaar, four thousand shops of spice, lantern, carpet, gold and ceramic, where haggling is not optional but expected.

Four Seasons Hotel Istanbul at Sultanahmet
Breakfast

Day 9 | Eating Istanbul

A day built around food rather than monuments. The Spice Bazaar first — the 17th-century Egyptian Bazaar — for lokum in every flavour, Gaziantep pistachios, dried fruit, Turkish coffee, tea, cheese, olives and spice.

Then out into Eminönü on foot for the street food that actually defines the city: simit, the sesame ring bread; döner and dürüm; lahmacun and pide; içli köfte; midye dolması, the stuffed mussels sold from trays on the pavement; and baklava to finish, with coffee served alongside a glass of water and a piece of lokum.

The afternoon crosses the Galata Bridge into Karaköy, up to the Galata Tower and along İstiklal Avenue. The evening is a Bosphorus dinner cruise, sailing between Europe and Asia with the palaces and mosques lit along both shores.

Four Seasons Hotel Istanbul at Sultanahmet
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 10 | Departure

A relaxed final morning — a hammam at the hotel’s Kurna Spa, a last Turkish coffee, or one more run at the Grand Bazaar — then a private transfer to Istanbul Airport.

Note

This journey runs on ferry schedules

Two crossings shape the itinerary. Athens to Santorini runs four to five hours by high-speed ferry. Chios to Çešme is only 18 kilometres and about twenty minutes, with up to five sailings a day, but it operates seasonally — and the Santorini to Chios leg usually connects through another island or by air. All of it is booked well in advance, and the season you travel in genuinely affects what is possible. April to October is the workable window.

Documents

A Schengen visa for Greece and an e-Visa for Türkiye

  • Passport — Valid for at least 150 days beyond arrival to satisfy the stricter of the two countries’ rules, with at least two blank pages.
  • Greece — A Schengen visa is required for many nationalities, applied for at the consulate of the country where you spend the most nights.
  • Türkiye — An e-Visa is available online in advance for many nationalities; some passports require a consular visa instead.
  • Leaving Schengen by sea — The Chios–Çešme crossing is a full international border. Carry your passport, not a copy, and expect exit and entry stamps on both sides.
  • Two currencies — Euro in Greece, Turkish lira in Türkiye. Carry cash for street food and bazaars.
  • We handle the paperwork — We complete and submit your visa, eVisa or travel-authorisation applications on your behalf. You supply the documents once; we do the filing, track the approval and hold the confirmations for you.
  • Before you travel — Entry rules change frequently and without much notice. Your specialist confirms the current requirements for every passport in your party at the time of booking, and checks them again before you fly.
