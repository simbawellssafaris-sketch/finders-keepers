Overview

Coexistence of
Nature, Art & Architecture

Opened in 1995 as an expansion of Tadao Ando's original Benesse House Museum, Oval sits at the highest point on the property, reached only by a private monorail climbing through the hillside.

The building takes the form of a vast ellipse — 40 metres along its long axis, 20 along its short — wrapped around a central courtyard pool that Ando conceived as a kind of liquid sculpture, framed by a portico usable as an open-air gallery. Just four double rooms and two suites make up the entire building, each with floor-to-ceiling windows onto the Seto Inland Sea and walls hung with drawings and installations commissioned directly from the artists whose work fills the wider museum below. Guests staying at Oval, along with those in the Museum building, receive a genuinely rare privilege: 24-hour access to wander the Benesse House Museum's galleries after hours, in pyjamas if they wish, with almost no other visitors in sight.

Not recommended for young children. To preserve the atmosphere of staying inside a working art museum, Benesse House Oval and Museum cannot accommodate children aged five and under.

Experiences

A Pumpkin, Turrell's Light

Naoshima's art sits everywhere — inside the museum, along the beaches, and scattered through the island's own working village.

Signature

24-Hour Museum Access

Oval and Museum guests can wander the Benesse House Museum's galleries after closing, often with almost no one else present.

Landmark

Yayoi Kusama's Pumpkin

One of Naoshima's most photographed works sits at the end of a nearby pier, recreated after storm damage in 2021.

Underground

Chichu Art Museum

Ando's museum built mostly underground to preserve the landscape above it, a short shuttle ride from Oval.

Village

Art House Project

Seven ordinary houses in Naoshima's residential Honmura district have been transformed into individual artworks, including James Turrell's Minamidera.

Bathing

Location

Naoshima,
the Art Island

Benesse House Oval sits at the highest point of Naoshima, an island of roughly 13km circumference in the Seto Inland Sea, reached by ferry from Okayama or Kagawa prefectures.

  • Reached by a private monorail climbing from the Museum building up to Oval's hillside position.
  • Naoshima sits roughly 13km north of Takamatsu, Kagawa Prefecture, in the Seto Inland Sea.
  • Ferries connect Naoshima to both Uno Port (Okayama) and Takamatsu Port (Kagawa).
  • The wider Benesse Art Site includes the Chichu Art Museum, Lee Ufan Museum and the Art House Project.
Fly

Okayama or Takamatsu airports connect onward to the respective ferry ports for Naoshima.

Ferry

Regular ferries run from Uno Port (Okayama) or Takamatsu Port (Kagawa) to Naoshima's Miyanoura port.

Arrive

A shuttle bus or short taxi ride connects the port to Benesse House, with the final climb to Oval made by private monorail.
