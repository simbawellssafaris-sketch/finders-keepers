OverviewThis ten-day circuit moves through the three great water ecosystems of northern Botswana — the crystal channels of the Okavango Delta, the riverine forests of Moremi, and the elephant-massed banks of Chobe — with a spectacular day excursion to Victoria Falls. Designed for those who want the complete Botswana experience, it balances aerial views from hot air balloons and helicopters, boat safaris through papyrus-lined waterways, and legendary overland game drives into one seamless, all-inclusive journey.

Itinerary

Gaborone

Day 1 | Arrival in Gaborone — Welcome to Botswana

Touch down at Sir Seretse Khama International Airport in Gaborone. A {{brand}} representative greets you at arrivals and escorts you to your private guide transfer. The drive to the Protea Hotel by Marriott Gaborone Masa Square takes approximately twenty minutes through the city's wide boulevards and modern architecture. Check into your suite — spacious, elegantly appointed, with floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the city.

This evening is dedicated to relaxation after your international journey. Your safari guide and local representative join you in the hotel's rooftop lounge for a welcome briefing — maps spread on the table, stories of the Delta ahead, the mood of anticipation building. A welcome dinner is served at the hotel's signature restaurant, featuring Botswana cuisine with a modern touch. Before dinner, a complimentary welcome cocktail is served at the Masa Square wellness centre, where your spa therapist also notes your preferences for the 90-minute restorative massage scheduled for your return to Gaborone on Day 10.

Dinner
Okavango Delta

Day 2 | Gaborone → Okavango Delta — Arrival at Sandibe

After breakfast, your chauffeur transfers you to Gaborone Airport for a commercial flight to Maun — approximately ninety minutes over the flat expanse of the Kalahari Basin. At Maun, your private light aircraft charter pilot is waiting. The flight to &Beyond Sandibe Okavango Safari Lodge is an experience in itself — thirty minutes of low-level flying over the Delta's geometric channels, papyrus islands, and wandering elephant herds. The Delta from the air is a revelation: a vast fan of crystal water spreading across the ancient Kalahari sand, each channel a silver thread connecting islands of green.

Sandibe is one of Botswana's most celebrated lodges — recently rebuilt as an architectural masterpiece designed to resemble a giant pangolin curled among the forest trees. Arrive in time for lunch on the open deck overlooking the seasonal floodplain. Your first game drive departs at 4:00 PM as the afternoon cools — elephant herds emerging from the thicket, the light softening to gold. A sundowner on the floodplain, the Delta's water channels reflecting the orange sky. Return to the lodge for a bonfire dinner under a sky so dark the Milky Way is visible from edge to edge.

&Beyond Sandibe Okavango Safari Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 3 | Okavango Delta — Hot Air Balloon Safari & Helicopter to Tsodilo Hills

Wake at 4:30 AM. Coffee is delivered to your suite. Transfer to the balloon launch site as the sky shifts from black to deep indigo. The balloon lifts silently above the Delta just as the first light catches the water channels below. For one hour you drift over the floodplains — elephant herds moving in single file, hippos returning to the deep channels, the geometric patterns of the Delta's waterways laid out below like a living map. After landing, a champagne bush breakfast is served on the floodplain: white linen, crystal glasses, a full breakfast prepared over an open fire in the middle of the wilderness.

Mid-morning, board a private helicopter for a flight to Tsodilo Hills — Botswana's only UNESCO World Heritage Site, rising from the flat savannah like a whale breaching. Over 4,500 rock paintings decorate the cave walls, some over 20,000 years old, created by the San people who revered this landscape as the place of creation. Your guide leads a walking tour through the hills before a picnic lunch is served in the shade of the ancient rocks. Return to Sandibe in the late afternoon. A bonfire dinner is laid out on the floodplain.

&Beyond Sandibe Okavango Safari Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 4 | Okavango Delta — A Full Day on the Water & Land

After breakfast, embark on a motorised boat safari through the deep channels of the Delta's permanent waterways. The boat glides silently through papyrus beds, past pods of hippos with only eyes and nostrils above the surface, alongside African fish eagles perched on dead trees. Your guide explains the Delta's extraordinary hydrology — how water from the Angolan highlands travels 1,200 kilometres before fanning across this corner of the Kalahari. Red lechwe bound through the shallows; a sitatunga watches from the reeds.

Return to the lodge for lunch and a siesta in the afternoon heat. At 4:00 PM, a game drive across the Delta's islands focuses on predators — lions along the floodplain edges, leopards in the riverine forest, and if fortune allows, the elusive African wild dog hunting in coordinated silence. A sundowner as the sky turns orange over the channels. Return to the lodge for a bonfire dinner under the stars.

Moremi Game Reserve

Day 5 | Okavango Delta → Moremi — Arrival at Camp Okuti

After breakfast, a short fifteen-minute scenic flight takes you from Sandibe to Camp Okuti in the Moremi Game Reserve — offering one final aerial view of the Delta's channels before descending into Moremi's mopane forest and riverine woodland. Moremi is the eastern extension of the Okavango Delta and one of Africa's most biologically diverse reserves. Camp Okuti is a Ker & Downey property — intimate, eco-sensitive, designed to blend into the forest canopy.

Arrive for lunch on the open deck overlooking the floodplain. Your first Moremi game drive departs at 4:00 PM, the reserve famous for its leopard sightings in the riverine forest and its resident wild dog pack — one of the most successful hunting packs in Botswana. A sundowner on the floodplain. Return to camp for a bonfire dinner, the Southern Cross bright above you.

Camp Okuti (Ker & Downey)
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 6 | Moremi — Walking Safari & Boat Ride

After breakfast, embark on a guided walking safari with your armed ranger through Moremi's dry islands. The experience of being on foot in the bush — tracking animals by footprint, reading the behaviour of birds, smelling the wild sage crushed underfoot — transforms your relationship with the landscape entirely. Your guide explains how the seasonal floodplains shift each year, how mopane forest provides year-round food for elephant, and how leopards use the riverine thickets as both hunting ground and refuge. The pace is slow. The silence is profound.

Return to camp for lunch and rest through the midday heat. At 4:00 PM, a motorised boat safari through Moremi's permanent waterways — the boat navigating narrow channels past papyrus islands and lily-covered lagoons. Hippos in the deeper sections. Crocodiles on the banks. African fish eagles calling from the trees. Kingfishers diving. A sundowner on the water as the sun sets behind the papyrus beds. Return to camp for a bonfire dinner.

Chobe National Park

Day 7 | Moremi → Chobe — Flight of the Elephants

After breakfast, board a light aircraft charter to Savute Safari Lodge in the Chobe Reserve — approximately one hour crossing the northern Delta and the dry savannah of Chobe. The flight reveals the transition from water to land: lush riverine forests giving way to baobab-studded plains and the dry grasslands of the Chobe region. Savute is famous for its elephants — the area supports one of the largest elephant populations in Africa, and during the dry season, hundreds gather along the Savute Channel daily.

Arrive in time for lunch with uninterrupted views across the floodplain from the lodge deck. Your first Savute game drive departs at 4:00 PM — elephant herds moving along the channel, their red dust coating their skin, lions hunting the floodplain edges, hyenas patrolling the periphery. A sundowner on the channel bank as the elephants silhouette against the setting sun. Return to the lodge for a bonfire dinner.

Savute Safari Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 8 | Victoria Falls — The Smoke That Thunders

After an early breakfast, board a cross-border charter flight to Victoria Falls, Zimbabwe — approximately one hour, the Zambezi River visible below, the spray of the falls rising hundreds of metres before the falls themselves come into view. Upon arrival, your Zimbabwean guide escorts you to Victoria Falls National Park. The falls — known locally as Mosi-oa-Tunya, "The Smoke That Thunders" — are at 1,708 metres wide and 108 metres high the largest curtain of falling water on earth and one of the Seven Natural Wonders of the World.

Your guide leads you along the footpaths on the Zimbabwean side, each viewpoint revealing a different face of the falls — the Devil's Cataract, Main Falls, Rainbow Falls, the Eastern Cataract. The roar is constant. The ground vibrates. Rainbows form and dissolve in the spray. Lunch is served at the historic Victoria Falls Hotel — a colonial landmark whose terrace looks toward the gorge. The afternoon is yours to explore the craft markets or take tea at the hotel before your charter flight returns you to Savute in time for a sundowner and bonfire dinner at the lodge.

Savute Safari Lodge
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 9 | Chobe — Full Day Among the Elephants

Today belongs entirely to the elephants of Savute. After breakfast, embark on a full morning game drive along the Savute Channel — the dry season concentrating wildlife along the remaining water in extraordinary numbers. Your guide explains the Channel's unusual history: a river that flows and dries over decades, driven not by rainfall but by tectonic activity beneath the Kalahari. The Channel's most recent flow began in 2008, and the wildlife concentration it has created since is among the most spectacular in Africa.

Return to the lodge for lunch and a long siesta. At 4:00 PM, a final Savute game drive focuses on predators — lions hunting along the channel, leopards in the riverine thickets, hyenas preparing for their nocturnal patrols. A farewell sundowner on the channel bank — champagne and canapés as the sun sets over the African bush. Return to the lodge for a final bonfire dinner under the stars.

Gaborone

Day 10 | Chobe → Gaborone — Spa, Farewell Dinner & Departure

After breakfast, board your light aircraft charter to Maun, then connect to a commercial flight back to Gaborone — offering one final aerial view of the Delta's channels before the city appears below. Upon arrival, your private guide transfers you to the Protea Hotel by Marriott Gaborone Masa Square.

This afternoon, your pre-arranged 90-minute full-body restorative massage awaits at the Masa Square wellness centre. The therapist uses locally sourced Botswana ingredients — shea butter, marula oil, and essential oils — to soothe muscles that have spent ten days bouncing across the African bush. It is the perfect close to the journey. A farewell dinner at the hotel's rooftop restaurant brings the safari to a fitting end — the city lights spread below, your guide joining you one last time to celebrate what you have seen and experienced.

Your airport transfer is arranged to align precisely with your international departure time — whether your flight leaves late that evening, in the early hours, or the following morning. There is no rush. You leave Botswana when it is time to leave.

Journey Details

Minimum Age

Varies by camp, activity and national park

  • Camps and lodges — Most safari camps accept children from six years, with shared game-drive vehicles usually from eight to twelve. Many properties will take younger children on a privately booked vehicle.
  • Walking safaris — Guided walking safaris carry a firm minimum of fifteen or sixteen years across most reserves. This is set by the licensing authority, not the camp, and is never waived.
  • Gorilla and chimpanzee trekking — Uganda and Rwanda both enforce a strict minimum of fifteen years. It is a national park regulation and there are no exceptions.
  • Hot-air balloon safaris — Operators generally require children to be at least seven, and tall enough to brace unaided in the basket on landing.
  • Family and exclusive-use camps — A number of our camps run dedicated family units, child-minding and tailored activity programmes, and will relax their usual age limit for a sole-use booking.
  • Talk to us first — Age limits vary from property to property and from activity to activity. Speak to our experts before you book, and we will pre-arrange the details with each lodge around the ages of the children travelling with you.

Safari Duffel

Duffel, safari hat, vest and hooded jumper

  • Your duffel — A soft-sided {{brand}} duffel, sized for the 15 kg limit on the light aircraft that move you between camps. Rigid suitcases cannot be carried on those flights at all.
  • What is inside it — A branded {{brand}} safari hat, a safari vest, and a hooded jumper for cold dawn starts and balloon mornings.
  • Yours to keep — The bag and everything in it is a gift, not a loan. Nothing is returned at the end of your journey.
  • Packing around it — Soft luggage, neutral colours, and layers. Laundry is included at nearly every camp, which is why 15 kg goes further than it sounds.

Note

Water levels shape the Okavango Delta experience

The Delta's flood waters typically peak between June and August, affecting which activities (mokoro, boating, or 4x4 game drives) are available at each camp. Malaria prophylaxis is recommended.

  • Early starts — Safaris begin very early in the morning. Your guide will confirm what time you will be collected from your camp or lodge.
  • Tailoring this journey — If you would like this package with more or fewer days, arranged for a group, a family or a solo traveller, or with add-ons, reach out and we will tailor it for you.
  • Route changes — Your guide or driver may alter the route in certain circumstances, but every destination set out in the itinerary will be covered.

Documents

Visa-free for most nationalities

  • Passport — Valid for at least six months beyond your arrival date, with at least two blank pages for stamps.
  • Visa — Visa-free for up to 90 days for UK, EU, US, Canadian, Australian and most Commonwealth and SADC passport holders. Other nationalities apply through the Botswana eVisa portal before travel.
  • Travelling with children — An unabridged birth certificate showing both parents is required for every traveller under 18. Where a child travels with one parent only, or with neither, a sworn parental consent affidavit is also needed.
  • Light-aircraft weight declaration — Camp-to-camp flights in the Delta require the actual body weight of every passenger in advance. It is a safety calculation for small aircraft, not a formality, and we must submit it with your booking.
  • We file it for you — Whether you are flying in from overseas or travelling from elsewhere on the continent, we complete and submit your visa application on your behalf. You hand us your documents once. We do the filing, chase the approval, and carry the confirmations in your travel pack so nothing rests on you remembering a reference number at a counter.
  • Most of Africa is open to you — The great majority of our African destinations are now either visa-free or issue a visa on arrival, so an embassy queue is rarely in your future at all. Where a visa on arrival does apply, we prepare and lodge the paperwork ahead of you — you reach the desk with a finished file rather than a blank form and a biro.
  • Before you travel — Entry rules change frequently and without much notice. Your specialist confirms the current requirements for every passport in your party at the time of booking, and checks them again before you fly.

Guaranteed Departures

Private, tailor-made departures on your dates, or join a small group

  • Once confirmed, you travel — When your departure date is confirmed, it is guaranteed. We do not cancel, merge or reschedule a confirmed departure for commercial reasons, however the final numbers fall.
  • What confirms a departure — A departure becomes confirmed once the agreed number of travellers has been reached and paid for in line with the agreed payment schedule. Places held without payment do not count towards it.
  • If the minimum is not reached — Where a group departure has not reached its agreed numbers, we tell you early rather than late, and you choose: move to the next available date, continue at a revised small-group rate, or take a full refund of everything you have paid.
  • Solo journeys — Solo and tailor-made itineraries carry no group minimum at all. Your dates are confirmed the moment your deposit is received.
  • Visas — We prepare and submit your visa application on your behalf. Please note that the granting of a visa rests solely with the immigration authority of the country concerned, and is never guaranteed by us.
  • Medication — We arrange malaria prophylaxis, together with anti-nausea and anti-anxiety tablets, for your journey. Any prescription medicine should be confirmed with your own doctor before you travel — they remain the final authority on what is suitable for you.
  • Africa is not always hot — Despite the stereotype, Africa moves through distinct seasons and mornings at altitude can be genuinely cold. A hooded jumper is provided for cold conditions, and for every guest joining a hot-air balloon safari.
  • Dietary requirements — If you have strict dietary needs, please tell us at the time of booking. This includes vegans, guests in recovery, non-drinkers, and anyone following a prescription from their doctor.
  • Travel insurance — We facilitate travel insurance for our clients. If you are already covered, please say so during booking so we can take it into account in your final quote.
  • Tailoring your quote — Some of our inclusions — hot-air balloon safaris, for example — may not be what you want. Point these out and we will adjust your final offer accordingly.
  • The Maasai massage & spa recovery — Guests travelling the safari circuit by road rather than by air should expect long and tiring days. The rough terrain of the jungle gives the vehicle a constant jolting ride, known affectionately as the Maasai massage. A spa session is provided at the end of your safari to recover.
  • What to pack — Neutral colours are the rule on safari: khaki, olive, stone and brown. Avoid black and dark blue, which draw tsetse flies, and leave camouflage at home entirely — it is reserved for the military and illegal for civilians in several African countries. Bring layers for cold dawn game drives, long sleeves and trousers for dusk when the mosquitoes are out, closed walking shoes, a sun hat and sunglasses. Lodge dinners are smart-casual; nothing formal is needed.
  • Your safari duffel & kit — The duffel bag is used when flying into the parks, where luggage is limited to 15 kg per person. A branded {{brand}} safari hat and safari vest are also provided.
