Overview

Nineteen
Thirteen

A hundred and thirty rooms and suites behind the most recognisable roofline in Nice.

Henri Negresco built this in 1913 and lost it within a decade, which is the usual fate of people who build something this ambitious. The architect was Édouard-Jean Niermans, who had already done the Moulin Rouge and the Hôtel du Palais at Biarritz, and he gave Nice a white building under a pink dome that has been the emblem of the city ever since. The façade is a listed monument. So is the Salon Royal, a rotunda under a glass roof with a 16,000-crystal Baccarat chandelier originally commissioned for Tsar Nicholas II, who never took delivery. The 130 rooms are individually decorated, and the hotel’s art collection runs to some six thousand works.

It is also a museum. The hotel holds a private collection of roughly 6,000 works spanning five centuries, hung throughout the public rooms and corridors. Ask for the tour.
