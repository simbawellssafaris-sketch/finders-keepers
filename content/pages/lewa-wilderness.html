Overview

Where the
Conservancy Began

Lewa Wilderness was once simply the Craig family homestead — the same ranch land that, in the mid-1980s, became the founding ground of the Lewa Wildlife Conservancy's rhino protection work.

Just nine rooms make up the whole of Lewa Wilderness: six hillside cottages tucked into the slope above the Eastern Marania Valley, and three garden cottages set on the lawns near the main house. Every room carries the character of successive generations of Craig family management — stone-and-thatch architecture, log fireplaces, private verandas — rather than a single design brief. The 65,000-acre Lewa Wildlife Conservancy that surrounds the lodge is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Kenya's strongest populations of both black and white rhino, alongside the world's largest population of Grevy's zebra.

Owned and run by the Craig family since 1924. The Craig family has been on this land for a century, and Lewa Wilderness remains a genuinely family-run homestead rather than a branded lodge — guests are received, quite literally, as family.

Location

65,000 Acres,
A UNESCO Site

Lewa Wildlife Conservancy sits between the Mount Kenya highlands and the semi-arid savannah to the north, at an elevation between 4,500 and 6,000 feet.

  • A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2013, recognised for its pioneering rhino conservation work.
  • Around 90% of the world's Grevy's zebra population lives across the Lewa-Borana landscape.
  • The conservancy grew from a private rhino sanctuary in the 1980s into a 65,000-acre protected landscape today.
  • Habitat here ranges from open grassland and acacia forest to riverine woodland, gorges and montane forest.
