Overview

A River That
Stopped, and Returned

Leroo La Tau sits on cliffs more than ten metres above the Boteti River, the Okavango Delta's main outflow, which ran dry for two decades before flowing again in 2009.

When the Okavango's floodwaters declined through the 1980s, the Boteti receded past the lodge entirely, leaving only isolated waterholes where a small pod of hippo became landlocked and crocodiles took to riverbank caves. Record rainfall in 2009 reversed two decades of drought, and the river now flows below Leroo La Tau once more, drawing the zebra and wildebeest of the Makgadikgadi migration back to its banks each dry season. Twelve thatched, glass-fronted suites are raised on individual wooden platforms along the cliff edge, each with 270-degree views over the Boteti River Valley and the national park beyond.

Facing east, for sunrise. Leroo La Tau's elevated, east-facing position makes it one of the best places in Botswana to watch the sunrise — with the added chance of spotting the three white rhinos known to frequent the area.

Location

The Boteti,
on Makgadikgadi's Edge

Leroo La Tau sits on the western boundary of Makgadikgadi Pans National Park, 140km south-east of Maun, where the Boteti River forms a critical water source for the park's wildlife.

  • The Boteti River is the main outflow of the Okavango Delta, running roughly 250km south-east from Maun.
  • Zebra and wildebeest migrate to the river at the end of winter, forming part of Botswana's second-largest migration.
  • The lodge's elevated, east-facing position makes it a favoured spot for sunrise.
  • Owing to its remote position, the Makgadikgadi Pans themselves are visited only on longer, multi-night stays.
Fly

Light aircraft charters connect Maun to an airstrip near the lodge, roughly a 30-minute flight.

Drive

A road transfer of around two hours from Maun also reaches the lodge directly.

Combine

Leroo La Tau pairs naturally with a wider circuit through the Okavango Delta, Moremi and Chobe.

Migration Season

Follow the Zebra Home

From April to October, thousands of zebra and wildebeest complete their migration at the Boteti River — right below Leroo La Tau's clifftop suites.
