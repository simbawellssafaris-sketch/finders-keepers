Itinerary

Zagreb

Day 1 | Arrival in Zagreb

Land at Zagreb and transfer to the Esplanade, an Art Deco landmark opened in 1925 as a refuge for passengers stepping off the Orient Express. It has been putting up film stars, politicians and artists ever since, and the interiors have been kept impeccably.

The afternoon walks the capital: Ban Jelačić Square, the neo-Gothic cathedral, and the Dolac open-air market where the country’s produce arrives each morning.

Then Tkalčićeva Street for its cafés, through the Stone Gate — the only surviving medieval city gate — and up to St Mark’s Church, whose tiled roof carries the coats of arms of Croatia, Dalmatia and Slavonia. Dinner is Croatian, with local wine.

Dinner
Plitvice Lakes National Park

Day 2 | Zagreb → Plitvice Lakes

Two hours south through the Croatian countryside to Plitvice Lakes, a UNESCO World Heritage site and, in our view, non-negotiable on a first visit to Croatia.

Sixteen emerald lakes sit in a stepped chain, joined by waterfalls and threaded with wooden walkways that run directly over the water. You explore on foot, by the park’s electric boats across Kozjak — the largest lake — and by panoramic train.

The Lower Lakes hold the most dramatic cascade, and Veliki Slap, the Great Waterfall, drops 78 metres, the tallest in Croatia. Water, karst and moss have collaborated here to produce something that looks too deliberate to be natural.

Fenomen Plitvice Resort
Breakfast, Dinner
Split

Day 3 | Plitvice → Split

Three hours south, descending through increasingly dramatic country to the Adriatic and Split, the largest city on the Dalmatian coast.

The old town is built inside Diocletian’s Palace — a fourth-century fortress complex built as a retirement home for the Roman emperor, and still, seventeen centuries later, a working town. More than 220 Roman-era buildings within the walls now hold homes, shops and bars.

You see the Peristyle and its colonnade, the Cathedral of Saint Domnius — originally Diocletian’s mausoleum, and the oldest cathedral in the world still in use in its original structure — the Temple of Jupiter, the Golden Gate, and the Riva waterfront. Dinner over the Adriatic.

Hotel Park Split
Breakfast, Dinner

Day 4 | Split & the Adriatic

A private guided morning going deeper into the palace: the underground cellars, once used for storage and waste, are now among the best-preserved Roman structures anywhere, precisely because they were sealed and forgotten for centuries.

The afternoon is yours to choose. Option A walks or drives up Marjan, the forested hill that has been Split’s retreat for centuries, for views over the city, the islands and the sea.

Option B takes a private boat along the coast for swimming and snorkelling in very clear water. The evening is free for Split’s restaurants.

Hotel Park Split
Breakfast
Hvar

Day 5 | Split → Hvar

A fast ferry or private boat across to Hvar, about an hour and twenty minutes, with the Dalmatian islands passing on either side.

Check in at Palace Elisabeth in the heart of Hvar Town, a few steps from the main square and the waterfront. The afternoon explores the town: St Stephen’s Cathedral above one of the largest piazzas in Dalmatia, and the Venetian architecture that runs through the old centre.

At sunset you walk up to the Fortica, the Spanish Fortress above the town, for the view across Hvar and the Pakleni Islands as the light goes. It is one of the defining images of the Dalmatian coast.

Palace Elisabeth Hvar Hotel
Breakfast, Dinner
Pakleni Islands

Day 6 | Hvar & the Pakleni Islands

A full private boat day through the Pakleni Islands, the archipelago that sits just off Hvar Town — Jerolim, Stipanska and Marinkovac, and the hidden coves between them.

Swimming and snorkelling in water that is genuinely as clear as the photographs suggest, and lunch at a secluded beachside restaurant reachable only from the water. For anyone who wants it, the whole day upgrades to a private yacht with captain and onboard lunch.

Back to Hvar in the afternoon, and a sunset dinner over the harbour.

Palace Elisabeth Hvar Hotel
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner
Dubrovnik

Day 7 | Hvar → Dubrovnik

South to Dubrovnik — by fast catamaran in season, roughly three hours, or by a private boat and road combination outside it.

Check in at the Excelsior, on the water just beyond the old town walls, then begin on the Pearl of the Adriatic itself. Through the Pile Gate onto the Stradun, the marble-paved main street that is simultaneously the city’s square and its spine.

Onofrio’s 15th-century fountain still supplies water. The Rector’s Palace was the seat of the Republic of Ragusa, which once rivalled Venice at sea. Then the cathedral, its treasury, and the old harbour. Dinner over the Adriatic.

Hotel Excelsior Dubrovnik
Breakfast, Dinner

Day 8 | The Old City & the Walls

A private guided walk through the old town and the history of the Ragusan Republic, then the thing everyone comes for: the City Walls. They run 1.94 kilometres around the entire old town, reach 25 metres in places, and are considered the finest fortification system in Europe.

Minčeta Fortress is the highest point and the best view; Lovrijenac, across the water, is the fortress most visitors recognise as King’s Landing. Walk it early — there is no shade and it fills up.

The afternoon takes the cable car to the summit of Mount Srđ, for the old town from above, the Adriatic to the horizon and the Dalmatian islands strung out along the coast. Dinner in the old town.

Hotel Excelsior Dubrovnik
Breakfast
Elaphiti Islands

Day 9 | The Elaphiti Islands

A private boat northwest to the Elaphiti Islands. Lopud is car-free and holds the crescent of Šunj Beach. Šipan is the largest and greenest, with olive groves, vineyards and the summer residences of Dubrovnik’s nobility along the main village of Šipanska Luka.

Koločep — Kalamota — has the Blue Cave, where you can swim and snorkel, and quiet coves at Donje Čelo and Gornje Čelo for anyone who would rather not.

Swimming, snorkelling, pine forest and turquoise water, with a traditional Dalmatian lunch somewhere along the way. Back to Dubrovnik for a farewell dinner over the Adriatic.

Hotel Excelsior Dubrovnik
Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 10 | Departure

Breakfast at the hotel and, if your flight allows, a final coffee on the Stradun before the private transfer to Dubrovnik Airport.
