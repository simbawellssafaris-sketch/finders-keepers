Overview

Where the
Restoration Began

Motse — Setswana for village — was Tswalu's first camp, and remains the heart of one of South Africa's most ambitious conservation projects: 114,000 hectares of former cattle farmland returned to wilderness.

Nine legae, the Setswana word for home, sit in gardens landscaped with indigenous southern Kalahari plants, built from pale sandstone, red-sand clay and thatch beneath the Korannaberg mountains. The reserve's green Kalahari landscape defies the popular image of desert — a semi-arid grassland of rolling dunes, savannah and ancient mountains, home to habituated meerkat colonies, black rhino, and some of the best sightings anywhere of aardvark and pangolin. A dedicated guide, tracker and private vehicle come with every booking, and Tswalu holds the lowest guest-to-space ratio of any privately protected reserve in South Africa.

One of three Tswalu camps. The Motse sits alongside Tarkuni, the Oppenheimer family's former private homestead, and Loapi, a light-footprint tented camp — this page covers the Motse alone.

Location

South Africa's
Largest Private Reserve

Tswalu Kalahari Reserve spans 114,000 hectares of the Korannaberg mountains and surrounding grasslands in the Northern Cape, South Africa's largest privately owned game reserve.

  • 114,000 hectares (over 1,100 km²) in the Northern Cape, restored from farmland since 1998.
  • Trophy hunting was banned by the Oppenheimer family, who took over the reserve after founder Stephen Boler's death.
  • A member of National Geographic's Unique Lodges of the World and part of the Diamond Route.
  • Malaria-free, making it a genuine option for family safaris with children of any age.
