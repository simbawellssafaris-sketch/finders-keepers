Overview

Wild, Open,
Unlike the Rest of Bali

"When we first saw the site, we felt it was wild, open, dry and rough, and quite unlike the rest of Bali," said WOHA co-founder Richard Hassell of the limestone plateau his firm transformed into one of Indonesia's most awarded resorts.

Built mainly from locally sourced materials, including limestone quarried from the site itself and recycled railway sleepers, the resort's 64 villas and residences follow an environmentally sustainable framework drawing on Balinese and Majapahit architectural tradition, filtered through a distinctly modern lens. Flat lava rock roofs absorb heat and bamboo ceilings encourage natural airflow, reducing reliance on air conditioning across the property's open-plan pavilions. Every villa includes a private pool and 24-hour Villa Host service, while the resort's 25-metre cliff-edge infinity pool, floating at the edge of a 100-metre limestone drop to the Indian Ocean, remains one of the most photographed features in Balinese hospitality.

Indonesia's first EarthCheck-certified design. Alila Villas Uluwatu was the first resort in Indonesia to receive EarthCheck's highest Best Practice Building Planning and Design certification, reflecting its commitment to sustainability without compromising on design.

Location

The Bukit Peninsula,
Bali's Southern Tip

Alila Villas Uluwatu sits atop limestone cliffs on Bali's southernmost tip, the Bukit Peninsula, an area first discovered by surfers decades ago and now one of the island's most exclusive addresses.

  • Perched on an elevated plateau above the cliffside of Uluwatu, spanning 35 acres.
  • Roughly a 30-minute drive from Ngurah Rai International Airport.
  • Within 10 to 15 minutes of Bali's best-known surf breaks and white sand beaches.
  • A short distance from Uluwatu Temple, one of Bali's most sacred sea temples.
